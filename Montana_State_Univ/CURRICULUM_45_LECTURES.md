## 📘 UNIT 1: FOUNDATIONS & LINEAR EQUATIONS (LECTURES 01 ~ 15)

### Week 1: Algebraic Language, Arithmetic & Evaluating Expressions
- **Lecture 01 (Mon) • Section 1.0:** Introduction to M090 & The Language of Algebra
  - *Key Concepts:* Variables, Constants, Expressions vs. Equations, Order of Operations (PEMDAS).
  - *Slide Focus:* 8 slides. Basic algebraic translations & PEMDAS hierarchy.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $24 \div 4 \cdot 2 = 12 \quad \text{(Left to Right, not } 24 \div 8\text{!)}$.
- **Lecture 02 (Wed) • Section 1.0:** Arithmetic Review: Fractions & Signed Numbers
  - *Key Concepts:* Adding/subtracting fractions with unlike denominators, multiplying & dividing fractions, division by zero ($a/0$ vs $0/a$).
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Board work problems (Workbook p.4, problems 1–14).
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $\dfrac{1}{8} + \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{3+4}{24} = \dfrac{7}{24}, \quad \dfrac{16}{0} = \text{Undefined}, \quad \dfrac{0}{16} = 0$.
- **Lecture 03 (Fri) • Section 1.1:** Evaluating Algebraic Expressions
  - *Key Concepts:* Substituting negative values with mandatory parentheses, order of operations with absolute values and exponents.
  - *Slide Focus:* 8 slides. Workbook p.6 (Examples 1, 2, 3).
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $\text{For } d=-2, f=5: \dfrac{d^2 - f^2}{d^2 + f^2} = \dfrac{(-2)^2 - (5)^2}{(-2)^2 + (5)^2} = \dfrac{4 - 25}{4 + 25} = -\dfrac{21}{29}$.

### Week 2: Translating Words, Exponents & Monomials
- **Lecture 04 (Mon) • Section 1.1:** Translating English Phrases into Algebraic Expressions
  - *Key Concepts:* Keywords for operations ("less than" reversal rule, "quotient", "sum", "difference").
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.7 translation drills.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $\text{"Six less than three times a number"} \implies 3x - 6 \quad (\text{NOT } 6 - 3x)$.
- **Lecture 05 (Wed) • Section 1.2:** Simplifying Monomials: Product & Quotient Rules
  - *Key Concepts:* Exponent properties: $x^m \cdot x^n = x^{m+n}$, $\dfrac{x^m}{x^n} = x^{m-n}$, Power to a power $(x^m)^n = x^{mn}$.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.8–9 (Examples 1, 2).
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $(3x^4 y^2)(-5x^3 y^7) = -15 x^{4+3} y^{2+7} = -15x^7 y^9$.
- **Lecture 06 (Fri) • Section 1.2:** Zero & Negative Exponents
  - *Key Concepts:* The zero exponent rule ($x^0 = 1$ for $x \neq 0$), negative exponent definition ($x^{-n} = \frac{1}{x^n}$), moving across fraction bars.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.9–11 (Examples 3, 4, 5).
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $\left(\dfrac{2a^{-3}b^4}{6a^2 b^{-2}}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{b^6}{3a^5}\right)^2 = \dfrac{b^{12}}{9a^{10}}$.

### Week 3: Polynomial Operations & Rational Expressions
- **Lecture 07 (Mon) • Section 1.3:** Adding and Subtracting Polynomials & The Distributive Property
  - *Key Concepts:* Identifying like terms, distributing negative signs through parentheses, perimeter applications.
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.12–13.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $(4x^2 - 3x + 7) - (2x^2 + 5x - 8) = 4x^2 - 3x + 7 - 2x^2 - 5x + 8 = 2x^2 - 8x + 15$.
- **Lecture 08 (Wed) • Section 1.4:** Multiplying Polynomials (FOIL & Special Products)
  - *Key Concepts:* Monomial $\times$ polynomial, binomial $\times$ binomial (FOIL), difference of squares $(a-b)(a+b)$, binomial squares $(a \pm b)^2$.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.14–15.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $(2x - 5)(3x + 4) = 6x^2 + 8x - 15x - 20 = 6x^2 - 7x - 20$.
- **Lecture 09 (Fri) • Section 1.5:** Adding and Subtracting Rational Expressions (Part 1: Like Denominators)
  - *Key Concepts:* Combining numerators over common denominators, distributing subtraction to the whole numerator polynomial.
  - *Slide Focus:* 8 slides. Workbook p.16 (Examples 1, 2).
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $\dfrac{3x+1}{x-4} - \dfrac{x+9}{x-4} = \dfrac{(3x+1)-(x+9)}{x-4} = \dfrac{2x-8}{x-4} = \dfrac{2(x-4)}{x-4} = 2$.

### Week 4: Rational Expressions with Unlike Denominators & Solving Linear Equations
- **Lecture 10 (Mon) • Section 1.5:** Adding and Subtracting Rational Expressions (Part 2: Unlike Denominators)
  - *Key Concepts:* Finding the Least Common Denominator (LCD), building equivalent fractions, simplifying results.
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.17 (Examples 3, 4).
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $\dfrac{5}{2x} + \dfrac{3}{x^2} = \dfrac{5x}{2x^2} + \dfrac{6}{2x^2} = \dfrac{5x+6}{2x^2}$.
- **Lecture 11 (Wed) • Section 1.6:** Solving Basic & Multi-Step Linear Equations
  - *Key Concepts:* Addition and multiplication properties of equality, combining like terms, variables on both sides.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.18–19 (Examples 1, 2).
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $3(2x - 4) + 5 = 4(x + 1) - 3 \implies 6x - 7 = 4x + 1 \implies 2x = 8 \implies x = 4$.
- **Lecture 12 (Fri) • Section 1.6:** Special Cases: Identities & Contradictions (No Solution vs. All Real Numbers)
  - *Key Concepts:* Infinite solutions (identity: $0=0$) vs. No solution (contradiction: $0=5$).
  - *Slide Focus:* 8 slides. Workbook p.19.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $2(x+3) = 2x + 6 \implies 6=6 \ (\text{Identity, } \mathbb{R}), \quad 3x - 1 = 3x + 4 \implies -1 = 4 \ (\text{Contradiction, } \emptyset)$.

### Week 5: Fractional Equations, Formulas, Inequalities & Unit 1 Capstone
- **Lecture 13 (Mon) • Section 1.7:** Solving Linear Equations with Fractions (The LCD Clearing Method)
  - *Key Concepts:* Multiplying every term by the LCD to eliminate all denominators in one step.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.20–22.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $\dfrac{x-2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{x+4}{6} \xrightarrow{\times 6} 2(x-2) + 3(1) = 1(x+4) \implies 2x - 1 = x + 4 \implies x = 5$.
- **Lecture 14 (Wed) • Section 1.8:** Solving Formulas for a Specified Variable (Literal Equations)
  - *Key Concepts:* Rearranging geometric & scientific formulas ($A = lw$, $P = 2l + 2w$, $C = \frac{5}{9}(F - 32)$, $I = Prt$).
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.23–24.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{Solve for } w: P = 2l + 2w \implies P - 2l = 2w \implies w = \dfrac{P - 2l}{2}$.
- **Lecture 15 (Fri) • Section 1.9 & Unit 1 Review:** Solving Linear Inequalities & Interval Notation
  - *Key Concepts:* Inequality symbols, number line graphing, interval notation $[a, b)$, and the **golden rule**: flip the inequality when multiplying/dividing by a negative!
  - *Slide Focus:* 12 slides. Workbook p.25–26 + Unit 1 Mastery Recap.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* $-3x + 7 \le 19 \implies -3x \le 12 \implies x \ge -4 \implies [-4, \infty)$.

## 📗 UNIT 2: GRAPHING, LINES & FUNCTIONS (LECTURES 16 ~ 30)

### Week 6: Intro to Graphing, Parent Equations & Domain/Range
- **Lecture 16 (Mon) • Section 2.0:** The Rectangular Coordinate Plane & Plotting Points
  - *Key Concepts:* The 4 quadrants, origin $(0,0)$, plotting $(x, y)$, checking whether a point satisfies an equation.
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.29–30.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{Plot } A(4, 0), B(-1, 3), C(0, 5), D(-3, -4) \text{ and identify quadrant/axis location.}
- **Lecture 17 (Wed) • Section 2.0:** Graphs of Basic Equations (Linear, Quadratic, Absolute Value, Cubic)
  - *Key Concepts:* Point plotting method, recognizing shapes ($y=|x|$ V-shape, $y=x^2$ U-shape parabola, $y=x^3$ S-curve).
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.30–31.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* y = |x| \implies (-2, 2), (-1, 1), (0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 2).
- **Lecture 18 (Fri) • Section 2.0:** Determining Domain and Range from Graphs in Interval Notation
  - *Key Concepts:* Domain (all valid $x$-values, left to right), Range (all valid $y$-values, bottom to top), open circles vs. solid circles.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.31.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{For } y = x^2: \text{Domain} = (-\infty, \infty), \ \text{Range} = [0, \infty).

### Week 7: Linear Equations in Two Variables & Intercepts
- **Lecture 19 (Mon) • Section 2.1:** Slope-Intercept Form of a Line ($y = mx + b$)
  - *Key Concepts:* Identifying slope $m$ and $y$-intercept $(0, b)$, converting standard form $Ax + By = C$ into slope-intercept form.
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.32–33.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* 3x + 2y = 8 \implies 2y = -3x + 8 \implies y = -\dfrac{3}{2}x + 4 \ (m = -\frac{3}{2}, \ b=4).
- **Lecture 20 (Wed) • Section 2.1:** Graphing Using Intercepts
  - *Key Concepts:* Finding $x$-intercept (set $y=0$) and $y$-intercept (set $x=0$), the cover-up method for standard form.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.34–35.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* 4x - 5y = 10 \implies x\text{-int: } (2.5, 0), \ y\text{-int: } (0, -2).
- **Lecture 21 (Fri) • Section 2.1:** Horizontal and Vertical Lines
  - *Key Concepts:* Horizontal line equation $y = c$ (slope $m=0$, no $x$-intercept), vertical line equation $x = c$ (slope undefined, no $y$-intercept).
  - *Slide Focus:* 8 slides. Workbook p.36–37.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* y = -2 \ (\text{Horizontal, } m=0), \quad x = 4 \ (\text{Vertical, } m \text{ undefined}).

### Week 8: Slope, Rates of Change & Point-Slope Form
- **Lecture 22 (Mon) • Section 2.2:** Slope Between Two Points
  - *Key Concepts:* Slope formula $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$, interpreting positive, negative, zero, and undefined slopes.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.38–39.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* m = \dfrac{7 - (-3)}{-2 - 4} = \dfrac{10}{-6} = -\dfrac{5}{3}.
- **Lecture 23 (Wed) • Section 2.2:** Slopes of Parallel and Perpendicular Lines
  - *Key Concepts:* Parallel lines have equal slopes ($m_1 = m_2$), perpendicular lines have negative reciprocal slopes ($m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1 \implies m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$).
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.40.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{If } m_1 = \dfrac{3}{4}, \text{ then } m_\parallel = \dfrac{3}{4} \text{ and } m_\perp = -\dfrac{4}{3}.
- **Lecture 24 (Fri) • Section 2.3:** Point-Slope Form of a Line
  - *Key Concepts:* Point-slope formula $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$, writing equation given a point and a slope, converting to slope-intercept form.
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.41–42.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{Given } m=-3, \ (2, 5): y - 5 = -3(x - 2) \implies y - 5 = -3x + 6 \implies y = -3x + 11.

### Week 9: Writing Line Equations & Introduction to Functions
- **Lecture 25 (Mon) • Section 2.3:** Writing Line Equations from Two Points & Geometric Conditions
  - *Key Concepts:* Two-step workflow (find slope $m$ first, then use point-slope form), lines parallel or perpendicular to a given line.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.43–44.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{Line through } (6, 3) \perp \text{ to } y = -\dfrac{2}{5}x + 1 \implies m = \dfrac{5}{2} \implies y = \dfrac{5}{2}x - 12.
- **Lecture 26 (Wed) • Section 2.4:** Introduction to Relations, Inputs, Outputs & Function Definition
  - *Key Concepts:* Relations as sets of ordered pairs, mapping diagrams, definition of a function (each input has EXACTLY ONE output).
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.45.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \{(1, 5), (2, 8), (3, 5)\} \implies \text{Function! } \quad \{(1, 5), (1, 9)\} \implies \text{NOT a function!}
- **Lecture 27 (Fri) • Section 2.4:** The Vertical Line Test & Identifying Functions Graphically
  - *Key Concepts:* Applying the Vertical Line Test (VLT) to graphs, circles, parabolas, and piecewise plots.
  - *Slide Focus:* 8 slides. Workbook p.46.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{A graph represents a function if and only if no vertical line intersects it more than once.}

### Week 10: Function Notation, Linear Functions & Real-World Modeling
- **Lecture 28 (Mon) • Section 2.5:** Function Notation: Evaluating $f(x)$
  - *Key Concepts:* Understanding $f(x)$ as the output $y$ for input $x$, evaluating linear and quadratic functions for numerical and variable inputs.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.47–48.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{Let } g(x) = -2x + 7, \ h(x) = 3x - 5. \text{ Evaluate } g(-1) = -2(-1) + 7 = 9.
- **Lecture 29 (Wed) • Section 2.6:** Linear Functions & Graphical Evaluation
  - *Key Concepts:* Reading $f(a) = b$ from a graph, finding $x$ when $f(x) = k$, expressing lines as $f(x) = mx + b$.
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.51–52.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{Given graph of } h(x): \ h(2) = -1, \quad \text{Solve } h(x) = -3 \implies x = 4.
- **Lecture 30 (Fri) • Section 2.7 & Unit 2 Review:** Applications of Linear Functions & Modeling (Montana Context)
  - *Key Concepts:* Interpreting slope as a rate of change and $y$-intercept as an initial value. Real-life models: car salesperson commission ($3 \text{ cars} \to \$760$, $5 \text{ cars} \to \$920$) and elevation vs. boiling point of water at Bozeman's "M" trail ($0\text{ ft} \to 212^\circ\text{F}$, $5700\text{ ft} \to 201^\circ\text{F}$).
  - *Slide Focus:* 12 slides. Workbook p.53–56 + Unit 2 Review.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* B(E) = -0.00193E + 212 \implies \text{At Bozeman "M" } (5700\text{ ft}): B(5700) \approx 201^\circ\text{F}.

## 📙 UNIT 3: QUADRATIC FUNCTIONS & EQUATIONS (LECTURES 31 ~ 45)

### Week 11: Introduction to Quadratics, The Vertex & Axis of Symmetry
- **Lecture 31 (Mon) • Section 3.0:** Intro to Quadratic Functions ($f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$)
  - *Key Concepts:* Quadratic standard form, parabola shape, opening direction ($a > 0 \implies$ opens upward, $a < 0 \implies$ opens downward).
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.59–60.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* f(x) = -2x^2 + 4x - 5 \implies a = -2 < 0 \ (\text{Opens downward, Maximum value}).
- **Lecture 32 (Wed) • Section 3.1:** Finding the Vertex ($x = -\frac{b}{2a}$) & Axis of Symmetry
  - *Key Concepts:* Calculating the $x$-coordinate of vertex $h = -\frac{b}{2a}$, substituting to find $k = f(h)$, Axis of Symmetry equation $x = h$.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.63–64.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 8 \implies x = -\dfrac{-6}{2(1)} = 3, \ f(3) = (3)^2 - 6(3) + 8 = -1 \implies \text{Vertex: } (3, -1).
- **Lecture 33 (Fri) • Section 3.1:** Finding the $y$-Intercept & Max/Min Applications
  - *Key Concepts:* Calculating $y$-intercept $(0, c)$, determining maximum or minimum value from the vertex $y$-coordinate.
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.64–65.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{Vertex } (3, -1) \text{ with } a = 1 > 0 \implies \text{Minimum value is } -1 \text{ at } x = 3.

### Week 12: Solving Quadratics: Square Root Property & GCF Factoring
- **Lecture 34 (Mon) • Section 3.2:** The Square Root Property: Basic Forms ($x^2 = k$)
  - *Key Concepts:* The fundamental square root property: $x^2 = k \implies x = \pm\sqrt{k}$, isolating $x^2$ before taking roots.
  - *Slide Focus:* 9 slides. Workbook p.66–67.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* 3x^2 - 27 = 0 \implies 3x^2 = 27 \implies x^2 = 9 \implies x = \pm 3.
- **Lecture 35 (Wed) • Section 3.2:** Square Root Property with Binomial Squares ($(ax + b)^2 = k$)
  - *Key Concepts:* Solving $(x - h)^2 = k \implies x - h = \pm\sqrt{k}$, isolating $x$, simplifying radical answers.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.68–69.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* 2(x - 3)^2 = 32 \implies (x - 3)^2 = 16 \implies x - 3 = \pm 4 \implies x = 7 \text{ or } x = -1.
- **Lecture 36 (Fri) • Section 3.3:** Finding Intercepts by Factoring: Greatest Common Factor (GCF)
  - *Key Concepts:* Zero Product Property ($ab = 0 \implies a = 0 \text{ or } b = 0$), identifying and factoring out GCF from quadratics missing a constant term ($c=0$).
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.70–73.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* 3x^2 - 12x = 0 \implies 3x(x - 4) = 0 \implies x = 0 \text{ or } x = 4.

### Week 13: Factoring Trinomials & Finding $x$-Intercepts
- **Lecture 37 (Mon) • Section 3.4 Part 1:** Factoring Trinomials with $a = 1$ ($x^2 + bx + c$)
  - *Key Concepts:* Finding two integers that multiply to $c$ and add to $b$, writing factors $(x + p)(x + q) = 0$, finding $x$-intercepts as points $(x, 0)$.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.74–75.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* x^2 - 7x + 12 = 0 \implies (x - 3)(x - 4) = 0 \implies x\text{-intercepts: } (3, 0) \text{ and } (4, 0).
- **Lecture 38 (Wed) • Section 3.4 Part 2:** Factoring Trinomials with $a \neq 1$ (AC Method & Grouping)
  - *Key Concepts:* AC method: multiply $a \cdot c$, find factors that add to $b$, split the middle term, factor by grouping.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.76–77.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* 2x^2 + 7x + 3 = 0 \ (a \cdot c = 6 \to 1 \cdot 6) \implies 2x^2 + 6x + x + 3 = 2x(x+3) + 1(x+3) = (2x+1)(x+3) = 0.
- **Lecture 39 (Fri) • Section 3.4 Part 2:** Factoring with a Common Monomial First & Difference of Squares
  - *Key Concepts:* Rule #1 of factoring: ALWAYS check for a GCF first! Difference of squares factoring $a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)$.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.77–78.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* 4x^2 - 36 = 0 \implies 4(x^2 - 9) = 4(x - 3)(x + 3) = 0 \implies x = \pm 3.

### Week 14: The Quadratic Formula & The Discriminant
- **Lecture 40 (Mon) • Section 3.5:** The Quadratic Formula ($x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$)
  - *Key Concepts:* Memorizing and applying the formula, identifying $a, b, c$ from standard form, mandatory parentheses around negative values.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.79–80.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0 \implies x = \dfrac{-(-4) \pm \sqrt{(-4)^2 - 4(1)(-5)}}{2(1)} = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 + 20}}{2} = \dfrac{4 \pm 6}{2} = 5, -1.
- **Lecture 41 (Wed) • Section 3.5:** The Discriminant & Radical Simplification
  - *Key Concepts:* The discriminant $D = b^2 - 4ac$ ($D > 0 \implies 2 \text{ real roots}$, $D = 0 \implies 1 \text{ real root}$, $D < 0 \implies \text{no real roots}$), simplifying non-perfect square radicals.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.81–82.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* x = \dfrac{6 \pm \sqrt{20}}{2} = \dfrac{6 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2} = 3 \pm \sqrt{5}.
- **Lecture 42 (Fri) • Section 3.6:** Strategic Decision Matrix: Choosing the Best Method
  - *Key Concepts:* Flowchart for selecting: Square Root vs. GCF vs. Factoring Trinomials vs. Quadratic Formula.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.83–85.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{Decision Rule: If } b=0 \implies \text{Square Root}; \text{ If } c=0 \implies \text{GCF}; \text{ If factorable } \implies \text{Trinomial}; \text{ Else } \implies \text{Formula.}

### Week 15: Complete Parabola Graphing & Course Capstone
- **Lecture 43 (Mon) • Section 3.6:** Finding All 5 Key Features of a Parabola
  - *Key Concepts:* 1) Direction of opening, 2) Vertex, 3) Axis of symmetry, 4) $y$-intercept, 5) $x$-intercepts.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.85–86.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{Summary Table: Vertex } (h, k), \text{ Axis } x=h, \ y\text{-int } (0, c), \ x\text{-int } (x_1, 0), (x_2, 0).
- **Lecture 44 (Wed) • Section 3.7:** Graphing Quadratic Functions Step-by-Step
  - *Key Concepts:* Plotting the vertex, sketching the axis of symmetry, plotting intercepts and symmetric points, drawing the smooth U-curve.
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Workbook p.87–91.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* f(x) = -(x - 1)^2 + 4 \implies \text{Vertex } (1, 4), \ y\text{-int } (0, 3), \ x\text{-ints } (-1, 0), (3, 0).
- **Lecture 45 (Fri) • Final Capstone:** M090 Mastery Celebration & Prof. Park and Sora's Send-off
  - *Key Concepts:* Connecting Unit 1 (Linear basics) $\to$ Unit 2 (Graphs & lines) $\to$ Unit 3 (Quadratics). Mathematical confidence for College Algebra (M121)!
  - *Slide Focus:* 10 slides. Comprehensive recap, congratulatory messages, and next steps for Gallatin College students.
  - *LaTeX Highlight:* \text{From } ax + b = 0 \implies y = mx + b \implies y = ax^2 + bx + c: \text{You have mastered the language of algebra!}
