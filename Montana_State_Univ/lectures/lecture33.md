## Slide 1: Section 3.0 Example 7A & 7B: Evaluating at -1

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 33 • Section 3.0 Example 7A, B (Workbook p. 62)

### Problem
### Example 7A & 7B (Workbook p. 62)
Let $\mathbf{g(x) = -x^2 + 2x + 7}$ and $\mathbf{h(x) = 3(x - 2)^2 - 5}$. Evaluate:
- **A.** $g(-1)$
- **B.** $h(-1)$
Use proper notation and simplify completely.

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\textbf{A. } g(-1) & = -(-1)^2 + 2(-1) + 7 \\
& = -(1) - 2 + 7 = -1 - 2 + 7 = \mathbf{4} \implies (-1, 4) \\[0.8em]
\textbf{B. } h(-1) & = 3(-1 - 2)^2 - 5 = 3(-3)^2 - 5 \\
& = 3(9) - 5 = 27 - 5 = \mathbf{22} \implies (-1, 22)
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**The minus sign outside the square:** $-(-1)^2 = -(1) = -1$. The negative sign is OUTSIDE the base, so it stays negative!

### Script
[Prof. Park] In Example 7A, watch $-(-1)^2$. Square $-1$ first to get $+1$, then apply the negative sign to get $-1$.

[TA Sora] So $-1 - 2 + 7 = 4$! And for $h(-1)$, $(-3)^2 = 9$, times 3 is 27, minus 5 is 22!

## Slide 2: Section 3.0 Example 7C & 7D: Evaluating at Zero & Y-Intercepts

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 33 • Section 3.0 Example 7C, D (Workbook p. 62)

### Problem
### Example 7C & 7D (Workbook p. 62)
Let $\mathbf{g(x) = -x^2 + 2x + 7}$ and $\mathbf{h(x) = 3(x - 2)^2 - 5}$. Evaluate:
- **C.** $h(0)$
- **D.** $g(0)$
Notice what both functions have in common!

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\textbf{C. } h(0) & = 3(0 - 2)^2 - 5 = 3(-2)^2 - 5 = 3(4) - 5 = 12 - 5 = \mathbf{7} \implies \mathbf{(0, 7)} \\[0.8em]
\textbf{D. } g(0) & = -(0)^2 + 2(0) + 7 = 0 + 0 + 7 = \mathbf{7} \implies \mathbf{(0, 7)} \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Insight: }} & \mathbf{\text{Both parabolas cross the } y\text{-axis at the EXACT SAME point } (0, 7)!}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Zero in vertex form:** In $h(x)$, plugging in $0$ gives $(-2)^2 = 4$, times 3 is 12, minus 5 is 7. Do not assume the answer is $-5$!

### Script
[Prof. Park] Look at that fascinating result! Both $g(0)$ and $h(0)$ equal 7.

[TA Sora] Both parabolas pass through the exact same $y$-intercept $(0, 7)$! Look at them intersecting on the coordinate plane.

## Slide 3: Section 3.0 Example 7E: Fractional Input h(5/2)

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 33 • Section 3.0 Example 7E (Workbook p. 62)

### Problem
### Example 7E (Workbook p. 62)
Let $\mathbf{h(x) = 3(x - 2)^2 - 5}$. Evaluate:
$$\mathbf{h\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)}$$
- Subtract $\frac{5}{2} - 2$ using a common denominator.
- Square the fraction.
- Multiply by 3 and subtract 5.

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
h\left(\frac{5}{2}\right) & = 3\left(\frac{5}{2} - 2\right)^2 - 5 = 3\left(\frac{5}{2} - \frac{4}{2}\right)^2 - 5 \\[0.5em]
& = 3\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 - 5 = 3\left(\frac{1}{4}\right) - 5 \\[0.5em]
& = \frac{3}{4} - 5 = \frac{3}{4} - \frac{20}{4} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{h\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)} & = \mathbf{-\frac{17}{4} = -4.25}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Squaring fractions:** $(\frac{1}{2})^2 = \frac{1}{4}$. Square both numerator and denominator!

### Script
[Prof. Park] In 7E, $\frac{5}{2} - 2 = \frac{1}{2}$. Squared gives $\frac{1}{4}$. Times 3 gives $\frac{3}{4}$.

[TA Sora] And $\frac{3}{4} - 5 = -\frac{17}{4} = -4.25$! Solid fraction arithmetic.

## Slide 5: Section 3.0 Example 7G: Binomial Input g(x - 6)

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 33 • Section 3.0 Example 7G (Workbook p. 62)

### Problem
### Example 7G (Workbook p. 62)
Let $\mathbf{g(x) = -x^2 + 2x + 7}$. Evaluate and simplify:
$$\mathbf{g(x - 6)}$$
- Replace $x$ with $(x - 6)$.
- Expand $(x - 6)^2 = x^2 - 12x + 36$.
- Distribute the negative sign and combine like terms.

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
g(x - 6) & = -(x - 6)^2 + 2(x - 6) + 7 \\[0.5em]
& = -(x^2 - 12x + 36) + 2x - 12 + 7 \\[0.5em]
& = -x^2 + 12x - 36 + 2x - 12 + 7 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{g(x - 6)} & = \mathbf{-x^2 + 14x - 41}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Distribute negative to all 3 terms:** $-(x^2 - 12x + 36) = -x^2 + 12x - 36$. Watch the middle sign flip to $+12x$!

### Script
[Prof. Park] In 7G, expand $(x - 6)^2$ first, then negate all three terms: $-x^2 + 12x - 36$.

[TA Sora] Then add $2x - 12 + 7$. Combining like terms gives $-x^2 + 14x - 41$!

## Slide 6: Section 3.0 Example 7H: Binomial Input h(k + 1)

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 33 • Section 3.0 Example 7H (Workbook p. 62)

### Problem
### Example 7H (Workbook p. 62)
Let $\mathbf{h(x) = 3(x - 2)^2 - 5}$. Evaluate and simplify:
$$\mathbf{h(k + 1)}$$
- Simplify inside the parentheses first: $(k + 1 - 2)$.
- Square the simplified binomial.
- Distribute 3 and subtract 5.

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
h(k + 1) & = 3((k + 1) - 2)^2 - 5 \\[0.5em]
& = 3(k - 1)^2 - 5 \quad (\text{combine inside first!}) \\[0.5em]
& = 3(k^2 - 2k + 1) - 5 \\[0.5em]
& = 3k^2 - 6k + 3 - 5 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{h(k + 1)} & = \mathbf{3k^2 - 6k - 2}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Simplify inside parentheses first:** Notice how $(k + 1 - 2)$ simplifies to $(k - 1)$ immediately, saving you from a much messier expansion!

### Script
[Prof. Park] Smart algebraists simplify inside parentheses first: $k + 1 - 2 = k - 1$.

[TA Sora] Squaring $(k - 1)$ gives $k^2 - 2k + 1$. Multiply by 3 to get $3k^2 - 6k + 3$, then minus 5 gives $3k^2 - 6k - 2$!

## Slide 7: The Negative Base Trap: -x^2 vs (-x)^2

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 33 • Section 3.0 Pitfall Alert

### Problem
### Why Students Lose Points on Negative Squares
Evaluate both expressions when $x = 3$ and when $x = -3$:
- **Case 1:** $-x^2$
- **Case 2:** $(-x)^2$
Explain the difference using order of operations.

### Solution
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|c|} 
\hline
\textbf{Expression} & x = 3 & x = -3 \\
\hline
-x^2 & -(3^2) = -(9) = \mathbf{-9} & -((-3)^2) = -(9) = \mathbf{-9} \\
\hline
(-x)^2 & (-3)^2 = \mathbf{+9} & (-(-3))^2 = (3)^2 = \mathbf{+9} \\
\hline
\end{array}$$

### Key Note
**Order of operations rule:** Exponents come BEFORE negation! In $-x^2$, you square $x$ first, then apply the negative sign. It is ALWAYS negative (or zero)!

### Script
[Prof. Park] Sora, this is the single most common error on Unit 3 quizzes.

[TA Sora] Yes! In $-x^2$, the negative sign sits outside the square. No matter what real number you plug in, $-x^2$ is ALWAYS negative!
