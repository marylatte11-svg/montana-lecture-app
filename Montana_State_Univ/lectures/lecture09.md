### [Slide 1] Section 1.5: Adding & Subtracting Rational Expressions (Part 1)
*Unit 1 • Lecture 09 • Section 1.5 (Workbook p. 16)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Rational Expressions Addition & Subtraction Principle
$$\frac{A}{C} + \frac{B}{C} = \frac{A + B}{C} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{A}{C} - \frac{B}{C} = \frac{A - B}{C}$$
- Fractions can **ONLY be added or subtracted if they share a Common Denominator (LCD)**.
- If denominators differ, find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of numbers and highest power of variables.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Find LCD of all denominators.} \\
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Multiply numerator and denominator of each fraction by missing factor.} \\
\text{Step 3: } & \text{Combine numerators over the single common denominator.} \\
\text{Step 4: } & \text{Simplify if possible.}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Warning:** NEVER add denominators! $\frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{y} = \frac{2}{y}$, NOT $\frac{2}{2y}$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Lecture 09! Today we open to Section 1.5 on page 16 of the workbook: Adding and Subtracting Rational Expressions.

[TA Sora] Just like in 4th grade arithmetic, you cannot add fractions until they have the same denominator! Let's examine Example 1 on Slide 2.

### [Slide 2] Example 1A & 1B: Basic Numerical & Algebraic Fractions
*Unit 1 • Lecture 09 • Section 1.5 Example 1A & 1B (Workbook p. 16)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Find the sum or difference (Workbook p. 16)
$$\text{A. } \frac{1}{6} + \frac{3}{4}$$
$$\text{B. } \frac{7}{y} - \frac{x}{y}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & \text{Denominators are } 6 \text{ and } 4. \quad \text{LCD} = 12. \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 2}{6 \cdot 2} + \frac{3 \cdot 3}{4 \cdot 3} = \frac{2}{12} + \frac{9}{12} = \mathbf{\frac{11}{12}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & \text{Denominators are already identical: } y. \\
&= \frac{7 - x}{y} = \mathbf{\frac{7 - x}{y}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Note:** In Part B, $7 - x$ cannot be simplified further. Do not cancel the $y$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1A, the LCD between 6 and 4 is 12. $\frac{2}{12} + \frac{9}{12} = \frac{11}{12}$.

[TA Sora] And in 1B, the denominators are already the same ($y$), so we simply combine numerators: $\frac{7 - x}{y}$!

### [Slide 3] Example 2A & 2B: Monomial Common Denominators
*Unit 1 • Lecture 09 • Section 1.5 Example 2A & 2B (Workbook p. 16)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Add or subtract by obtaining a common denominator (Workbook p. 16)
$$\text{A. } \frac{5}{w} - \frac{7}{2w}$$
$$\text{B. } \frac{1}{6x} + \frac{2}{9x}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & \text{Denominators: } w, 2w \implies \text{LCD} = 2w. \\
&= \frac{5 \cdot 2}{w \cdot 2} - \frac{7}{2w} = \frac{10}{2w} - \frac{7}{2w} = \mathbf{\frac{3}{2w}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & \text{Denominators: } 6x, 9x. \quad \text{LCM}(6, 9) = 18 \implies \text{LCD} = 18x. \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 3}{6x \cdot 3} + \frac{2 \cdot 2}{9x \cdot 2} = \frac{3}{18x} + \frac{4}{18x} = \mathbf{\frac{7}{18x}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**LCD Check:** For $6x$ and $9x$, don't use $54x$! The least common multiple of 6 and 9 is 18, so $\text{LCD} = 18x$.

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 2A, the LCD of $w$ and $2w$ is $2w$. Multiply the first fraction by $\frac{2}{2}$ to get $\frac{10 - 7}{2w} = \frac{3}{2w}$.

[TA Sora] In 2B, the smallest number both 6 and 9 divide into is 18. So the LCD is $18x$. Result: $\frac{3 + 4}{18x} = \frac{7}{18x}$!

### [Slide 4] Example 2C & 2D: Two-Variable Denominators
*Unit 1 • Lecture 09 • Section 1.5 Example 2C & 2D (Workbook p. 16)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Add or subtract by obtaining a common denominator (Workbook p. 16)
$$\text{C. } \frac{3}{y} + \frac{5}{x}$$
$$\text{D. } \frac{11y}{2x} - \frac{x}{5y}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & \text{Denominators: } y, x \implies \text{LCD} = xy. \\
&= \frac{3 \cdot x}{y \cdot x} + \frac{5 \cdot y}{x \cdot y} = \mathbf{\frac{3x + 5y}{xy}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & \text{Denominators: } 2x, 5y \implies \text{LCD} = 10xy. \\
&= \frac{11y \cdot 5y}{2x \cdot 5y} - \frac{x \cdot 2x}{5y \cdot 2x} = \frac{55y^2}{10xy} - \frac{2x^2}{10xy} = \mathbf{\frac{55y^2 - 2x^2}{10xy}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Variable Multiplication Trap:** In 2D, $11y \cdot 5y = 55y^2$ and $x \cdot 2x = 2x^2$. Don't forget the squared exponents on the variables!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 2C, the denominators share no common factors, so the LCD is simply their product: $xy$. Result: $\frac{3x + 5y}{xy}$.

[TA Sora] And in 2D, the LCD is $10xy$. When we multiply $11y$ by $5y$, we get $55y^2$. Result: $\frac{55y^2 - 2x^2}{10xy}$.

### [Slide 5] Example 2E & 2F: Mixed Constant & Variable Denominators
*Unit 1 • Lecture 09 • Section 1.5 Example 2E & 2F (Workbook p. 16)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Add or subtract by obtaining a common denominator (Workbook p. 16)
$$\text{E. } \frac{2}{7r} + \frac{3}{7}$$
$$\text{F. } \frac{4}{5} - \frac{9}{5w}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ E:}\quad & \text{Denominators: } 7r, 7 \implies \text{LCD} = 7r. \\
&= \frac{2}{7r} + \frac{3 \cdot r}{7 \cdot r} = \frac{2}{7r} + \frac{3r}{7r} = \mathbf{\frac{2 + 3r}{7r}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ F:}\quad & \text{Denominators: } 5, 5w \implies \text{LCD} = 5w. \\
&= \frac{4 \cdot w}{5 \cdot w} - \frac{9}{5w} = \frac{4w}{5w} - \frac{9}{5w} = \mathbf{\frac{4w - 9}{5w}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Never Cancel Across Addition/Subtraction:** In Part E, you CANNOT cancel the $r$ in $3r$ with the $r$ in $7r$! Terms connected by $+$ or $-$ cannot be cancelled!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Look at Example 2E: $\frac{2}{7r} + \frac{3}{7}$. The LCD is $7r$. Multiplying the second term by $\frac{r}{r}$ gives $\frac{2 + 3r}{7r}$.

[TA Sora] And Sora's warning: you CANNOT cancel the $r$'s here! The 2 has no $r$, and addition blocks cancellation!

[Prof. Park] In 2F, the LCD is $5w$, giving $\frac{4w - 9}{5w}$.
