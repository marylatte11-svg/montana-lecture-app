### [Slide 1] Section 1.3: Adding & Subtracting Polynomials — Key Terms
*Unit 1 • Lecture 07 • Section 1.3 (Workbook p. 12)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Polynomial Terminology (Workbook p. 12)
- **Polynomial:** A monomial, or two or more monomials combined by addition or subtraction.
- **Monomial:** Exactly one term ($7x^2$, $-5$).
- **Binomial:** Exactly two terms ($3x + 4$, $y^2 - 9$).
- **Trinomial:** Exactly three terms ($ax^2 + bx + c$).
- **Like Terms:** Terms having the **exact same variable(s) and exponent(s)**. Only coefficients are added/subtracted.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Like Terms: } & 5x^2 \text{ and } 3x^2 \implies (5+3)x^2 = \mathbf{8x^2} \\
\text{Unlike Terms: } & 5x^2 \text{ and } 3x \implies \text{CANNOT be combined!}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Warning:** When adding like terms, NEVER add or change the exponents! $3x^2 + 5x^2 = 8x^2$, NOT $8x^4$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Lecture 07! Today we open to Section 1.3 on page 12 of your M090 Workbook: Adding and Subtracting Polynomials and the Distributive Property.

[TA Sora] Monomial = 1 term, Binomial = 2 terms, Trinomial = 3 terms. Like terms have the exact same variables and powers.

[Prof. Park] Remember: when you add like terms, you only add their coefficients. The powers never change!

### [Slide 2] Example 1A & 1B: Combining Like Terms
*Unit 1 • Lecture 07 • Section 1.3 Example 1A & 1B (Workbook p. 12)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Simplify the polynomial by combining like terms (Workbook p. 12)
$$\text{A. } 6y + 12 - 7y$$
$$\text{B. } 9m^2 - 24m + 12m - 32$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & 6y + 12 - 7y \\
&= (6y - 7y) + 12 \\
&= (6 - 7)y + 12 = \mathbf{-y + 12} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & 9m^2 - 24m + 12m - 32 \\
&= 9m^2 + (-24 + 12)m - 32 \\
&= \mathbf{9m^2 - 12m - 32}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Watch-out:** In Part B, $9m^2$ has exponent 2, while $-24m$ has exponent 1. They are NOT like terms! Do not combine them into $-15m$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Look at Example 1A: $6y + 12 - 7y$. Sora, which terms can we combine?

[TA Sora] The $y$-terms! $6y$ and $-7y$. $6 - 7 = -1$, so we get $-y + 12$.

[Prof. Park] Exactly. In 1B, only $-24m$ and $+12m$ are like terms. That gives $9m^2 - 12m - 32$.

### [Slide 3] Example 1C & 1D: Multi-Term Polynomial Simplification
*Unit 1 • Lecture 07 • Section 1.3 Example 1C & 1D (Workbook p. 12)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Simplify the polynomial by combining like terms (Workbook p. 12)
$$\text{C. } 3n^2 - 17n - n^2 + 4n$$
$$\text{D. } 5x^3 + 3x^2 - 2x - 9 - 12x^3 - 10x + 4$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & (3n^2 - n^2) + (-17n + 4n) \\
&= (3 - 1)n^2 + (-17 + 4)n = \mathbf{2n^2 - 13n} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & (5x^3 - 12x^3) + 3x^2 + (-2x - 10x) + (-9 + 4) \\
&= (5 - 12)x^3 + 3x^2 + (-2 - 10)x + (-5) \\
&= \mathbf{-7x^3 + 3x^2 - 12x - 5}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sign Error Alert:** Don't forget that $-n^2$ means $-1n^2$. So $3n^2 - 1n^2 = 2n^2$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] On Slide 3, group by descending powers. In 1C, $3n^2 - n^2 = 2n^2$, and $-17n + 4n = -13n$.

[TA Sora] In 1D, grouping cubic, quadratic, linear, and constant terms gives $-7x^3 + 3x^2 - 12x - 5$.

### [Slide 4] Example 2A & 2B: Adding & Subtracting Polynomial Groups
*Unit 1 • Lecture 07 • Section 1.3 Example 2A & 2B (Workbook p. 12)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Perform the indicated operation and simplify (Workbook p. 12)
$$\text{A. } (5x^3 + 2x^2 - 13x) + (4x^3 - 10x^2 + 9)$$
$$\text{B. } (2y^6 + y^4 - y) - (8y^6 - 3y^4 + 9y)$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A\ (Addition):}\quad & (5x^3 + 4x^3) + (2x^2 - 10x^2) - 13x + 9 \\
&= \mathbf{9x^3 - 8x^2 - 13x + 9} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B\ (Subtraction):}\quad & \text{Distribute the negative sign to every term in 2nd group!} \\
&= 2y^6 + y^4 - y - 8y^6 + 3y^4 - 9y \\
&= (2y^6 - 8y^6) + (y^4 + 3y^4) + (-y - 9y) \\
&= \mathbf{-6y^6 + 4y^4 - 10y}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**THE BIGGEST EXAM TRAP IN 1.3:** In Part B, distribute the minus to all terms! $-(8y^6 - 3y^4 + 9y) = -8y^6 + 3y^4 - 9y$. Watch the $+3y^4$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 2B, notice that minus sign between the groups. Sora, what must every student do first?

[TA Sora] Distribute the negative sign! Change every single sign inside the second group: $8y^6 \to -8y^6$, $-3y^4 \to +3y^4$, and $+9y \to -9y$!

[Prof. Park] Then combine like terms to get $-6y^6 + 4y^4 - 10y$.

### [Slide 5] Example 3A & 3B: Distributive Property with Monomials
*Unit 1 • Lecture 07 • Section 1.3 Example 3A & 3B (Workbook p. 13)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Perform the indicated operations and simplify fully (Workbook p. 13)
$$\text{A. } -2x(3x^2 + 8x - 4)$$
$$\text{B. } 2(8y - 5) + 7(-2y - 6)$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & (-2x)(3x^2) + (-2x)(8x) + (-2x)(-4) \\
&= -6x^{1+2} - 16x^{1+1} + 8x \\
&= \mathbf{-6x^3 - 16x^2 + 8x} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & [2(8y) - 2(5)] + [7(-2y) + 7(-6)] \\
&= (16y - 10) + (-14y - 42) \\
&= (16y - 14y) + (-10 - 42) = \mathbf{2y - 52}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Negative Monomial Multiplication:** In 3A, $(-2x)(-4) = +8x$. A negative times a negative is POSITIVE!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Turn to page 13, Example 3. In 3A, multiply $-2x$ across all three terms inside.

[TA Sora] Add exponents when multiplying: $x \cdot x^2 = x^3$, $x \cdot x = x^2$. And $(-2x)(-4) = +8x$.

[Prof. Park] In 3B, distribute $2$ and $7$, giving $16y - 10 - 14y - 42 = 2y - 52$.

### [Slide 6] Example 3C & 3D: Higher Degree Distribution
*Unit 1 • Lecture 07 • Section 1.3 Example 3C & 3D (Workbook p. 13)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Perform the indicated operations and simplify fully (Workbook p. 13)
$$\text{C. } -x(x^2 - 4) - 2(x^3 + 2x)$$
$$\text{D. } y(7y^2 + 4y - 9) - 3(8y^3 - 3y^2 - 4y)$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & [-x(x^2) - x(-4)] + [-2(x^3) - 2(2x)] \\
&= (-x^3 + 4x) - 2x^3 - 4x \\
&= (-x^3 - 2x^3) + (4x - 4x) = \mathbf{-3x^3} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & (7y^3 + 4y^2 - 9y) - 24y^3 + 9y^2 + 12y \\
&= (7y^3 - 24y^3) + (4y^2 + 9y^2) + (-9y + 12y) \\
&= \mathbf{-17y^3 + 13y^2 + 3y}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Notice Cancellation in 3C:** $4x - 4x = 0$. The linear terms completely cancel out!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Look at 3C: $-x^3 + 4x - 2x^3 - 4x$. The $+4x$ and $-4x$ cancel to zero, leaving $-3x^3$.

[TA Sora] In 3D, distributing $-3$ flips all signs to $-24y^3 + 9y^2 + 12y$. Combining gives $-17y^3 + 13y^2 + 3y$!

### [Slide 7] Example 3E & 3F: Nested Parentheses Simplification
*Unit 1 • Lecture 07 • Section 1.3 Example 3E & 3F (Workbook p. 13)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Simplify fully by clearing nested grouping symbols (Workbook p. 13)
$$\text{E. } 3 + 2(5x - 4(x - 9)) + 7x$$
$$\text{F. } 11x - 8(y + 5(y - 2(y - x)))$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ E:}\quad & \text{Innermost first: } -4(x - 9) = -4x + 36 \\
& 3 + 2(5x - 4x + 36) + 7x = 3 + 2(x + 36) + 7x \\
&= 3 + 2x + 72 + 7x = \mathbf{9x + 75} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ F:}\quad & \text{Innermost: } -2(y - x) = -2y + 2x \\
&= 11x - 8(y + 5(y - 2y + 2x)) \\
&= 11x - 8(y + 5(-y + 2x)) \\
&= 11x - 8(y - 5y + 10x) = 11x - 8(-4y + 10x) \\
&= 11x + 32y - 80x = \mathbf{-69x + 32y}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Order of Operations:** In 3E, NEVER add $3 + 2 = 5$ at the start! Multiplication takes precedence over addition!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 3E and 3F, work from the inside out! In 3E, distribute $-4$ into $(x - 9)$ first.

[TA Sora] Never do $3 + 2 = 5$ first—that breaks PEMDAS!

[Prof. Park] In 3F, peel each layer from inside out to reach $-69x + 32y$.
