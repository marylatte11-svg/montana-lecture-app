### [Slide 1] Section 1.4: Multiplying Polynomials & The FOIL Strategy
*Unit 1 • Lecture 08 • Section 1.4 (Workbook p. 14)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### The FOIL Method for Multiplying Two Binomials
$$(a + b)(c + d) = \underbrace{ac}_{\text{First}} + \underbrace{ad}_{\text{Outer}} + \underbrace{bc}_{\text{Inner}} + \underbrace{bd}_{\text{Last}}$$
- **Every term in the first polynomial** must multiply **every term in the second polynomial**.
- Combine like terms (typically the Outer and Inner products).

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Example: } & (x + 3)(x + 5) \\
&= x \cdot x + x \cdot 5 + 3 \cdot x + 3 \cdot 5 \\
&= x^2 + 5x + 3x + 15 = \mathbf{x^2 + 8x + 15}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Warning:** FOIL is just a memory aid for the distributive property. When multiplying a binomial by a trinomial, FOIL expands to 6 multiplications!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Lecture 08! Today we are on pages 14 and 15 of the workbook: Multiplying Polynomials.

[TA Sora] Everyone loves FOIL: First, Outer, Inner, Last! Let's conquer Example 1 on Slide 2!

### [Slide 2] Example 1A & 1B: Standard FOIL Binomial Multiplication
*Unit 1 • Lecture 08 • Section 1.4 Example 1A & 1B (Workbook p. 14)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Use the distributive property to multiply and fully simplify (Workbook p. 14)
$$\text{A. } (2x + 4)(3x + 5)$$
$$\text{B. } (x - 4)(5x - 1)$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & (2x)(3x) + (2x)(5) + (4)(3x) + (4)(5) \\
&= 6x^2 + 10x + 12x + 20 \\
&= \mathbf{6x^2 + 22x + 20} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & (x)(5x) + (x)(-1) + (-4)(5x) + (-4)(-1) \\
&= 5x^2 - x - 20x + 4 \\
&= \mathbf{5x^2 - 21x + 4}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Double Negative Trap:** In 1B, Last times Last is $(-4)(-1) = +4$. Watch your signs!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1A: First is $6x^2$, Outer is $10x$, Inner is $12x$, Last is $20$. $10x + 12x = 22x$, so $6x^2 + 22x + 20$.

[TA Sora] In 1B, watch the negative signs: $-x - 20x = -21x$, and $(-4)(-1) = +4$. Result: $5x^2 - 21x + 4$!

### [Slide 3] Example 1C & 1D: Binomial Products with Leading Constants
*Unit 1 • Lecture 08 • Section 1.4 Example 1C & 1D (Workbook p. 14)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Use the distributive property to multiply and fully simplify (Workbook p. 14)
$$\text{C. } 2(b - 3)(b + 4)$$
$$\text{D. } 4(w + 2)(w - 2)$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & \text{Step 1: FOIL the binomials first:} \\
& (b - 3)(b + 4) = b^2 + 4b - 3b - 12 = b^2 + b - 12 \\
& \text{Step 2: Distribute the outer 2:} \\
& 2(b^2 + b - 12) = \mathbf{2b^2 + 2b - 24} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & \text{Step 1: Difference of squares for } (w + 2)(w - 2): \\
& w^2 - 2w + 2w - 4 = w^2 - 4 \\
& \text{Step 2: Distribute the outer 4:} \\
& 4(w^2 - 4) = \mathbf{4w^2 - 16}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Strategy:** FOIL the binomials inside parentheses first, and distribute the constant factor last! Never distribute the constant into BOTH parentheses!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1C and 1D, we have a constant in front: $2(b - 3)(b + 4)$.

[TA Sora] Pro-tip: Multiply the two binomials first! $(b-3)(b+4) = b^2 + b - 12$. Then double everything: $2b^2 + 2b - 24$.

[Prof. Park] And in 1D, $(w+2)(w-2) = w^2 - 4$. Multiply by 4 gives $4w^2 - 16$.

### [Slide 4] Example 1E & 1F: Squaring a Binomial Trap
*Unit 1 • Lecture 08 • Section 1.4 Example 1E & 1F (Workbook p. 14)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Fully simplify each expression (Workbook p. 14)
$$\text{E. } (2y - 7)^2$$
$$\text{F. } 2(y - 7)^2$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ E:}\quad & \text{Write out as repeated multiplication:} \\
& (2y - 7)(2y - 7) = (2y)(2y) + (2y)(-7) + (-7)(2y) + (-7)(-7) \\
&= 4y^2 - 14y - 14y + 49 = \mathbf{4y^2 - 28y + 49} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ F:}\quad & \text{Expand } (y - 7)^2 \text{ first:} \\
& (y - 7)(y - 7) = y^2 - 14y + 49 \\
& \text{Now multiply by 2:} \\
& 2(y^2 - 14y + 49) = \mathbf{2y^2 - 28y + 98}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**THE DEADLIEST ALGEBRA SIN:** $(2y - 7)^2 \neq 4y^2 + 49$! Exponents DO NOT distribute over subtraction! You MUST write it twice and FOIL to get the middle term $-28y$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Slide 4 contains what I call the ultimate algebra trap: $(2y - 7)^2$.

[TA Sora] Please, everyone: NEVER just square the $2y$ and square the $7$! That kills the middle term! Write it as $(2y - 7)(2y - 7)$ and FOIL to get $4y^2 - 28y + 49$.

[Prof. Park] In 1F, expand $(y - 7)^2 = y^2 - 14y + 49$ first, then multiply by 2 to get $2y^2 - 28y + 98$.

### [Slide 5] Example 1H & 1I: Multiplying Binomials by Trinomials
*Unit 1 • Lecture 08 • Section 1.4 Example 1H & 1I (Workbook p. 14)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Multiply and combine like terms (Workbook p. 14)
$$\text{H. } (w + 2)(w^2 - 2w + 9)$$
$$\text{I. } (2x - 1)(x^2 + 3x - 10)$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ H:}\quad & w(w^2 - 2w + 9) + 2(w^2 - 2w + 9) \\
&= (w^3 - 2w^2 + 9w) + (2w^2 - 4w + 18) \\
&= w^3 + (-2w^2 + 2w^2) + (9w - 4w) + 18 \\
&= \mathbf{w^3 + 5w + 18} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ I:}\quad & 2x(x^2 + 3x - 10) - 1(x^2 + 3x - 10) \\
&= (2x^3 + 6x^2 - 20x) + (-x^2 - 3x + 10) \\
&= 2x^3 + (6x^2 - x^2) + (-20x - 3x) + 10 \\
&= \mathbf{2x^3 + 5x^2 - 23x + 10}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Notice Quadratic Cancellation:** In Part H, $-2w^2 + 2w^2 = 0$. The $w^2$ terms completely vanish!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1H and 1I, distribute each term of the binomial across all three terms of the trinomial.

[TA Sora] In 1H, $w(w^2 - 2w + 9) + 2(w^2 - 2w + 9)$. The $w^2$ terms cancel out, leaving $w^3 + 5w + 18$!

[Prof. Park] In 1I, distributing $2x$ and $-1$ yields $2x^3 + 5x^2 - 23x + 10$.

### [Slide 6] Example 2 & 3: Perimeter & Area Applications
*Unit 1 • Lecture 08 • Section 1.4 Example 2 & 3 (Workbook p. 15)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Geometry Applications of Polynomials (Workbook p. 15)
A rectangle has length $L = 3x - 4$ and width $W = 2x + 7$.
- **Example 2:** Find the perimeter of the rectangle.
- **Example 3:** Find the area of the rectangle.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Example\ 2\ (Perimeter):}\quad P &= 2L + 2W \\
&= 2(3x - 4) + 2(2x + 7) \\
&= 6x - 8 + 4x + 14 = \mathbf{10x + 6} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Example\ 3\ (Area):}\quad A &= L \cdot W \\
&= (3x - 4)(2x + 7) \\
&= (3x)(2x) + (3x)(7) + (-4)(2x) + (-4)(7) \\
&= 6x^2 + 21x - 8x - 28 = \mathbf{6x^2 + 13x - 28}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Don't Mix Up Perimeter & Area:** Perimeter adds all 4 sides ($2L + 2W$, linear units). Area multiplies length by width ($L \cdot W$, square units)!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Now turn to page 15, Examples 2 and 3. We are given a rectangle with length $3x - 4$ and width $2x + 7$.

[TA Sora] Perimeter is the distance around: $2(3x - 4) + 2(2x + 7) = 6x - 8 + 4x + 14 = 10x + 6$.

[Prof. Park] And Area is length times width: FOIL $(3x - 4)(2x + 7) = 6x^2 + 13x - 28$.

### [Slide 7] Example 4: Modified Geometric Area Problem
*Unit 1 • Lecture 08 • Section 1.4 Example 4 (Workbook p. 15)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Modified Rectangle Dimensions (Workbook p. 15)
A given rectangle has length $2x + 3$ and width $x - 6$.
If the **length is decreased by 7** and the **width is increased by 12**, find the **area of the new rectangle** in terms of $x$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Find new dimensions:} \\
& L_{\text{new}} = (2x + 3) - 7 = \mathbf{2x - 4} \\
& W_{\text{new}} = (x - 6) + 12 = \mathbf{x + 6} \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Calculate new area: } A_{\text{new}} = L_{\text{new}} \cdot W_{\text{new}} \\
& A_{\text{new}} = (2x - 4)(x + 6) \\
&= 2x(x) + 2x(6) - 4(x) - 4(6) \\
&= 2x^2 + 12x - 4x - 24 = \mathbf{2x^2 + 8x - 24}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Check:** Update BOTH dimensions before multiplying! $(2x - 4)(x + 6)$, then FOIL to get $2x^2 + 8x - 24$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Example 4 tests both translation and polynomial multiplication. Length $2x+3$ decreased by 7 becomes $2x-4$. Width $x-6$ increased by 12 becomes $x+6$.

[TA Sora] Then multiply the new dimensions: $(2x - 4)(x + 6) = 2x^2 + 8x - 24$ square units!

[Prof. Park] Outstanding work! That completes all problems in Section 1.4.
