### [Slide 1] Section 2.3 Example 6: Parallel Line through (-1, 3)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 23 • Section 2.3 Example 6 (Workbook p. 42)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 6 (Workbook p. 42)
Find the equation of the line containing the point $(-1, 3)$ and **parallel** to the line:
$$\mathbf{2x + y = 10}$$
- Find the slope of the given line.
- Use the same slope for the parallel line.
- Write the new equation in slope-intercept form.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Given line: } & 2x + y = 10 \implies y = -2x + 10 \implies \mathbf{m = -2} \\[0.5em]
\text{Parallel line has: } & m = -2, \quad \text{point } (-1, 3) \\[0.5em]
y - y_1 & = m(x - x_1) \\[0.5em]
y - 3 & = -2(x - (-1)) = -2(x + 1) \\[0.5em]
y - 3 & = -2x - 2 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{-2x + 1}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Throw away the old intercept:** Only steal the SLOPE ($m = -2$) from the given line! Discard the $+10$ intercept!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 6, the given line has slope $-2$. Our parallel line also needs slope $-2$.

[TA Sora] Plugging $(-1, 3)$ and $m = -2$ into point-slope gives $y = -2x + 1$. Look at both lines running parallel on the grid!

### [Slide 2] Section 2.3 Example 7: Perpendicular Line through (2, -3)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 23 • Section 2.3 Example 7 (Workbook p. 42)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 7 (Workbook p. 42)
Find the equation of the line containing the point $(2, -3)$ and **perpendicular** to the line:
$$\mathbf{4y - x = 20}$$
- Solve for $y$ to find the slope of the given line.
- Take the opposite reciprocal for $m_\perp$.
- Write the equation in slope-intercept form.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Given line: } & 4y = x + 20 \implies y = \frac{1}{4}x + 5 \implies \mathbf{m_1 = \frac{1}{4}} \\[0.5em]
\text{Perpendicular slope: } & \mathbf{m_\perp = -4} \quad (\text{opposite reciprocal}) \\[0.5em]
y - (-3) & = -4(x - 2) \\[0.5em]
y + 3 & = -4x + 8 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{-4x + 5}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Opposite AND Reciprocal:** The reciprocal of $\frac{1}{4}$ is $4$, and making it opposite gives $-4$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 7, the original slope is $\frac{1}{4}$. The perpendicular slope must be $-4$.

[TA Sora] Using point $(2, -3)$ with $m = -4$, we get $y = -4x + 5$. They meet at a 90-degree right angle!

*Unit 2 • Lecture 23 • Section 2.3 Practice #1 (Workbook p. 43)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### In-Class Practice 1 (Workbook p. 43)
Find the equation of the line with a $y$-intercept of $(0, 5)$ and a slope of $m = -\frac{3}{5}$.
- Write the equation in slope-intercept form.
- Graph the line on the Cartesian coordinate plane.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Given: } & m = -\frac{3}{5}, \quad b = 5 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{-\frac{3}{5}x + 5}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Direct form:** When $y$-intercept $(0, 5)$ is given, $b = 5$ immediately!

### [Slide 4] In-Class Practice #2: X-intercept (5, 0) and m = 3/5
*Unit 2 • Lecture 23 • Section 2.3 Practice #2 (Workbook p. 43)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### In-Class Practice 2 (Workbook p. 43)
Find the equation of the line with $x$-intercept of $(5, 0)$ and a slope of $m = \frac{3}{5}$.
- Use point-slope form $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
- Graph the line on the Cartesian coordinate plane.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Point: } & (x_1, y_1) = (5, 0), \quad m = \frac{3}{5} \\[0.5em]
y - 0 & = \frac{3}{5}(x - 5) \\[0.5em]
y & = \frac{3}{5}x - \left(\frac{3}{5} \cdot 5\right) \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{\frac{3}{5}x - 3}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Don't assume b = 5:** $(5, 0)$ is an $x$-intercept! The $y$-intercept solves to $(0, -3)$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Practice 2, $(5, 0)$ is an $x$-intercept. Point-slope reveals $y = \frac{3}{5}x - 3$.

[TA Sora] Notice how the line crosses at $(5, 0)$ on the $x$-axis and $(0, -3)$ on the $y$-axis!

### [Slide 5] In-Class Practice #3: Through (-2, -3) and (6, 1)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 23 • Section 2.3 Practice #3 (Workbook p. 43)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### In-Class Practice 3 (Workbook p. 43)
Find the equation of the line that contains the points:
$$\mathbf{(-2, -3) \quad \text{and} \quad (6, 1)}$$
- Calculate the slope $m$.
- Use point-slope form to find the equation.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
m & = \frac{1 - (-3)}{6 - (-2)} = \frac{1 + 3}{6 + 2} = \frac{4}{8} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}} \\[0.5em]
y - 1 & = \frac{1}{2}(x - 6) \\[0.5em]
y - 1 & = \frac{1}{2}x - 3 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}x - 2}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Double negatives:** In the slope calculation, $-(-3) = +3$ and $-(-2) = +2$, giving $\frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Practice 3, the slope is $\frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$. Using point $(6, 1)$ gives $y = \frac{1}{2}x - 2$.

[TA Sora] Let's verify: does $(-2, -3)$ work? $\frac{1}{2}(-2) - 2 = -1 - 2 = -3$. It checks out perfectly!

### [Slide 6] In-Class Practice #4 & #5: Special Lines
*Unit 2 • Lecture 23 • Section 2.3 Practice #4 & #5 (Workbook pp. 43-44)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### In-Class Practice 4 & 5 (Workbook pp. 43–44)
Find the equations of the lines:
- **#4:** Points $(2, -9)$ and $(2, -3)$
- **#5:** Points $(5, -2)$ and $(4, -2)$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{\#4: } & \text{Both points have } x = 2 \implies \mathbf{x = 2} \quad (\text{Vertical line, } m = \text{undefined}) \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\#5: } & \text{Both points have } y = -2 \implies \mathbf{y = -2} \quad (\text{Horizontal line, } m = 0)
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Instant inspection:** When $x$-coordinates are the same, $x = c$. When $y$-coordinates are the same, $y = c$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Practice 4 and 5, always inspect the coordinates first. Matching $x$ means vertical $x=2$; matching $y$ means horizontal $y=-2$.

[TA Sora] HOY and VUX in action once again!

### [Slide 7] In-Class Practice #6: Parallel to 3x - y = 7 through (2, 1)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 23 • Section 2.3 Practice #6 (Workbook p. 44)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### In-Class Practice 6 (Workbook p. 44)
Find the equation of the line containing the point $(2, 1)$ and **parallel** to the line:
$$\mathbf{3x - y = 7}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Given line: } & -y = -3x + 7 \implies y = 3x - 7 \implies \mathbf{m = 3} \\[0.5em]
\text{Parallel line: } & m = 3, \quad \text{point } (2, 1) \\[0.5em]
y - 1 & = 3(x - 2) = 3x - 6 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{3x - 5}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Solving for y:** Don't forget $-y = -3x + 7$ means $y = +3x - 7$. The slope is $+3$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Practice 6, the slope is $+3$. Using point $(2, 1)$ with $m = 3$ gives $y = 3x - 5$.

[TA Sora] Both lines have slope 3 and run parallel forever!

### [Slide 8] In-Class Practice #7: Perpendicular to 2y - x = 20 through (6, 3)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 23 • Section 2.3 Practice #7 (Workbook p. 44)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### In-Class Practice 7 (Workbook p. 44)
Find the equation of the line containing the point $(6, 3)$ and **perpendicular** to the line:
$$\mathbf{2y - x = 20}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Given line: } & 2y = x + 20 \implies y = \frac{1}{2}x + 10 \implies \mathbf{m_1 = \frac{1}{2}} \\[0.5em]
\text{Perpendicular slope: } & \mathbf{m_\perp = -2} \\[0.5em]
y - 3 & = -2(x - 6) = -2x + 12 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{-2x + 15}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Reciprocal sign check:** Opposite reciprocal of $\frac{1}{2}$ is $-2$. Then $-2 \cdot (-6) = +12$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Practice 7, the original slope is $\frac{1}{2}$, making the perpendicular slope $-2$. With point $(6, 3)$, we get $y = -2x + 15$.
