### [Slide 1] Section 1.8: Solving Formulas for a Specified Variable
*Unit 1 • Lecture 14 • Section 1.8 (Workbook p. 23)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Literal Equations & Formulas (Workbook p. 23)
- **Literal Equation / Formula:** An equation containing **multiple variables** ($a, b, x, y, r, t$).
- **Solving for a Specified Variable:** Isolating that target variable completely on one side.
- **Strategy:** Treat all other letters exactly like known constants/numbers!
$$\text{If you would subtract 5, subtract } x\text{! If you would divide by 3, divide by } m\text{!}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Linear Model: } & 3x + y = 8 \implies y = 8 - 3x \\
\text{Multiplication Model: } & d = rt \implies r = \frac{d}{t}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Reminder:** The rules do NOT change just because letters are involved. Use the exact same properties of equality!

### [Slide 2] Example 1A & 1B: Solving Linear Equations for y
*Unit 1 • Lecture 14 • Section 1.8 Example 1A & 1B (Workbook p. 23)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve each formula for y (Workbook p. 23)
$$\text{A. } 3x + y = 8$$
$$\text{B. } 2x - 5y = 12$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & \text{Isolate } y \text{ by subtracting } 3x \text{ from both sides:} \\
& 3x + y - 3x = 8 - 3x \\
& \mathbf{y = 8 - 3x} \quad (\text{or } \mathbf{y = -3x + 8}) \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & \text{Step 1: Subtract } 2x: \\
& -5y = 12 - 2x \\
& \text{Step 2: Divide by } -5: \\
& y = \frac{12 - 2x}{-5} = \mathbf{\frac{2x - 12}{5}} \quad \left(\text{or } \mathbf{y = \frac{2}{5}x - \frac{12}{5}}\right)
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Dividing by a Negative:** In 1B, dividing by $-5$ flips the signs in the numerator: $\frac{12 - 2x}{-5} = \frac{2x - 12}{5}$. Both forms are correct!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1A: $3x + y = 8$. Subtract $3x$: $y = 8 - 3x$.

[TA Sora] In 1B: $2x - 5y = 12$. Subtract $2x$ to get $-5y = 12 - 2x$, then divide by $-5$: $y = \frac{2x - 12}{5}$ or $\frac{2}{5}x - \frac{12}{5}$.

### [Slide 3] Example 1C & 1D: Solving for Factored & Monomial Variables
*Unit 1 • Lecture 14 • Section 1.8 Example 1C & 1D (Workbook p. 23)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve each formula for y (Workbook p. 23)
$$\text{C. } Z = x + wxy$$
$$\text{D. } 5xy = 19$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & \text{Step 1: Subtract } x \text{ to isolate the } y\text{-term:} \\
& Z - x = wxy \\
& \text{Step 2: Divide both sides by the coefficients of } y \text{, which are } wx: \\
& \mathbf{y = \frac{Z - x}{wx}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & \text{Divide both sides by } 5x: \\
& \mathbf{y = \frac{19}{5x}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Multi-Variable Coefficients:** In 1C, $w$ and $x$ are multiplying $y$. To isolate $y$, divide by the entire package $wx$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1C, $Z = x + wxy$. First subtract $x$: $Z - x = wxy$. Then divide by $wx$ to isolate $y$: $y = \frac{Z - x}{wx}$.

[TA Sora] And in 1D, $5x$ is multiplying $y$. Divide by $5x$ to get $y = \frac{19}{5x}$.

### [Slide 4] Example 2A & 2B: Solving for Specified Variable b
*Unit 1 • Lecture 14 • Section 1.8 Example 2A & 2B (Workbook p. 23)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve each literal equation for the specified variable (Workbook p. 23)
$$\text{A. } a + b - c = d; \quad \text{solve for } b$$
$$\text{B. } ax^2 + bx + c = 0; \quad \text{solve for } b$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & \text{Subtract } a \text{ and add } c \text{ to both sides:} \\
& b = d - a + c \\
& \mathbf{b = d - a + c} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & \text{Step 1: Move all non-}b \text{ terms to the right side:} \\
& bx = -ax^2 - c \\
& \text{Step 2: Divide both sides by } x: \\
& \mathbf{b = \frac{-ax^2 - c}{x}} \quad \left(\text{or } \mathbf{b = -ax - \frac{c}{x}}\right)
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Treating Terms as Blocks:** In 2B, $ax^2$ and $c$ have no $b$. Move them as complete units over to the right side!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 2A, solve $a + b - c = d$ for $b$. Subtract $a$ and add $c$: $b = d - a + c$.

[TA Sora] In 2B, solve the standard quadratic form for $b$. Move $ax^2$ and $c$ to the right: $bx = -ax^2 - c$. Then divide by $x$: $b = \frac{-ax^2 - c}{x}$.

### [Slide 5] Example 2C & 2D: Solving Fractional Formulas for r
*Unit 1 • Lecture 14 • Section 1.8 Example 2C & 2D (Workbook p. 24)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve each formula for r (Workbook p. 24)
$$\text{C. } m = \frac{r - 7n}{5}; \quad \text{solve for } r$$
$$\text{D. } t = \frac{d}{r}; \quad \text{solve for } r$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & \text{Step 1: Multiply both sides by } 5: \\
& 5m = r - 7n \\
& \text{Step 2: Add } 7n \text{ to both sides:} \\
& \mathbf{r = 5m + 7n} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & \text{Target } r \text{ is in the denominator! Multiply by } r \text{ first:} \\
& r \cdot t = d \\
& \text{Now divide by } t: \\
& \mathbf{r = \frac{d}{t}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Variable in Denominator Trap:** In 2D, you CANNOT solve for $r$ while it is trapped in the bottom! Multiply by $r$ first to bring it upstairs: $rt = d \implies r = \frac{d}{t}$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 2C, clear the denominator 5 first: $5m = r - 7n \implies r = 5m + 7n$.

[TA Sora] And in 2D, the variable $r$ is trapped in the denominator! Never divide by $d$ while $r$ is downstairs. Multiply by $r$ first to get $rt = d$, then divide by $t$ to get $r = \frac{d}{t}$!

### [Slide 6] Example 2E & 2F: Geometric Formulas for W
*Unit 1 • Lecture 14 • Section 1.8 Example 2E & 2F (Workbook p. 24)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve each geometry formula for W (Workbook p. 24)
$$\text{E. } P = 2L + 2W; \quad \text{solve for } W$$
$$\text{F. } V = LWH; \quad \text{solve for } W$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ E\ (Perimeter):}\quad & \text{Step 1: Subtract } 2L \text{ from both sides:} \\
& P - 2L = 2W \\
& \text{Step 2: Divide by } 2: \\
& \mathbf{W = \frac{P - 2L}{2}} \quad \left(\text{or } \mathbf{W = \frac{P}{2} - L}\right) \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ F\ (Volume):}\quad & \text{Divide both sides by } LH: \\
& \mathbf{W = \frac{V}{LH}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Don't Cancel the 2 Illegally!** In Part E, $\frac{P - 2L}{2} \neq P - L$! The 2 divides the ENTIRE numerator, so it becomes $\frac{P}{2} - L$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 2E, $P = 2L + 2W$. Subtract $2L$ to get $P - 2L = 2W$, then divide by 2: $W = \frac{P - 2L}{2}$.

[TA Sora] And in 2F, $V = LWH$. Divide by the package $LH$ to get $W = \frac{V}{LH}$.

### [Slide 7] Section 1.8 Review: Solving Equations by Clearing Fractions
*Unit 1 • Lecture 14 • Section 1.8 Review A & B (Workbook p. 24)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### REVIEW: Solve the following equations by clearing fractions (Workbook p. 24)
$$\text{A. } \frac{y - 2}{5} = \frac{3}{2}y + \frac{4}{5}$$
$$\text{B. } \frac{2}{7} = \frac{3}{7}(x - 4) - \frac{5}{7}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Review\ A:}\quad & \text{Denominators: } 5, 2 \implies \mathbf{\text{LCD} = 10} \\
& 10 \cdot \left(\frac{y - 2}{5}\right) = 10 \cdot \left(\frac{3}{2}y\right) + 10 \cdot \left(\frac{4}{5}\right) \\
& 2(y - 2) = 15y + 8 \\
& 2y - 4 = 15y + 8 \implies -12 = 13y \implies \mathbf{y = -\frac{12}{13}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Review\ B:}\quad & \text{Denominators are all } 7 \implies \mathbf{\text{LCD} = 7} \\
& 7 \cdot \left(\frac{2}{7}\right) = 7 \cdot \left[\frac{3}{7}(x - 4)\right] - 7 \cdot \left(\frac{5}{7}\right) \\
& 2 = 3(x - 4) - 5 \\
& 2 = 3x - 12 - 5 \implies 2 = 3x - 17 \implies 19 = 3x \implies \mathbf{x = \frac{19}{3}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Multiplying Binomial Numerator:** In Review A, $10 \div 5 = 2$, which must distribute to $(y - 2)$ to make $2y - 4$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Page 24 ends with two cumulative review problems. In Review A, multiplying by LCD 10 gives $2(y - 2) = 15y + 8 \implies 2y - 4 = 15y + 8 \implies y = -\frac{12}{13}$.

[TA Sora] And in Review B, multiplying by 7 clears all denominators instantly: $2 = 3(x - 4) - 5 \implies 2 = 3x - 17 \implies x = \frac{19}{3}$!
