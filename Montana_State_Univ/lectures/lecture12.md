### [Slide 1] Section 1.6: Classifying Linear Equations (Part 2)
*Unit 1 • Lecture 12 • Section 1.6 (Workbook p. 19)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### The Three Types of Linear Equations (Workbook p. 19)
- **1. Conditional:** The equation has a **finite number of unique real solutions** (e.g. $x = 5$, $x = 0$). True under certain conditions.
- **2. Contradiction:** Variables cancel out leaving a **FALSE statement** (e.g. $0 = 7$). **NO SOLUTION ($\emptyset$)**.
- **3. Identity:** Variables cancel out leaving a **TRUE statement** (e.g. $5 = 5$, $0 = 0$). **ALL REAL NUMBERS ($\mathbb{R}$)**.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Conditional: } & 2x = 6 \implies x = 3 \quad (\text{One unique solution}) \\
\text{Contradiction: } & x + 1 = x + 4 \implies 1 = 4 \quad (\text{False! No solution}) \\
\text{Identity: } & 2(x + 1) = 2x + 2 \implies 2x + 2 = 2x + 2 \quad (\text{True for all } x!)
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Warning:** Don't confuse $x = 0$ (conditional, exactly 1 solution) with $0 = 5$ (contradiction, no solution)!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Lecture 12! Today we finish Section 1.6 on pages 18 and 19 of your workbook.

[TA Sora] Every linear equation in the universe falls into one of three buckets: Conditional (one answer), Contradiction (no solution), or Identity (all real numbers). Let's see Examples 2C and 2D on Slide 2!

### [Slide 2] Example 2C & 2D: Special Case Equations
*Unit 1 • Lecture 12 • Section 1.6 Example 2C & 2D (Workbook p. 18)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve each equation and classify (Workbook p. 18)
$$\text{C. } 2(7 - x) + 11 = 12 - 2x$$
$$\text{D. } -4 - (3 - x) - 2 = 9(x - 1) - 8x$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & 14 - 2x + 11 = 12 - 2x \\
& 25 - 2x = 12 - 2x \\
& 25 - 2x + 2x = 12 - 2x + 2x \\
& \mathbf{25 = 12} \quad \text{\textbf{FALSE statement!}} \\
& \implies \mathbf{\text{Contradiction (No Solution, } \emptyset\text{)}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & -4 - 3 + x - 2 = 9x - 9 - 8x \\
& x - 9 = x - 9 \\
& -9 = -9 \quad \text{\textbf{TRUE statement!}} \\
& \implies \mathbf{\text{Identity (All Real Numbers, } \mathbb{R}\text{)}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**What Happened to the Variable?** When the variable completely cancels out, look at the remaining numbers: False statement $\implies$ Contradiction. True statement $\implies$ Identity!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 2C, adding $2x$ to both sides cancels the variables completely, leaving $25 = 12$, which is impossible. That is a Contradiction: No Solution.

[TA Sora] In 2D, simplifying both sides gives $x - 9 = x - 9$, which is always true! That is an Identity: All Real Numbers.

### [Slide 3] In-Class Practice #1 & #2
*Unit 1 • Lecture 12 • Section 1.6 Extra Practice #1 & #2 (Workbook p. 19)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve and classify (Workbook p. 19)
$$\text{1. } 3x - 2 = -9x + 15$$
$$\text{2. } r - 3 - 7 + r = 10 + 2r$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Practice\ 1:}\quad & 3x + 9x = 15 + 2 \\
& 12x = 17 \implies \mathbf{x = \frac{17}{12}} \quad \mathbf{[Conditional]} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Practice\ 2:}\quad & (r + r) + (-3 - 7) = 10 + 2r \\
& 2r - 10 = 2r + 10 \\
& 2r - 2r = 10 + 10 \implies \mathbf{-10 = 10} \quad \text{\textbf{FALSE!}} \\
& \implies \mathbf{\text{Contradiction (No Solution, } \emptyset\text{)}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sign Watch in #2:** $-10$ does NOT equal $+10$! They are opposites, not equals. Hence it is a contradiction!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] On page 19, In-Class Practice #1: $3x - 2 = -9x + 15$. Add $9x$ and add 2 to get $12x = 17 \implies x = \frac{17}{12}$. That is Conditional.

[TA Sora] In Practice #2, $2r - 10 = 2r + 10$. Subtracting $2r$ leaves $-10 = 10$, which is false! Contradiction: No Solution.

### [Slide 4] In-Class Practice #3 & #4
*Unit 1 • Lecture 12 • Section 1.6 Extra Practice #3 & #4 (Workbook p. 19)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve and classify (Workbook p. 19)
$$\text{3. } 3(w - 4) = 3(w - 9) + 15$$
$$\text{4. } -4x + 4(x - 4) = -11$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Practice\ 3:}\quad & 3w - 12 = 3w - 27 + 15 \\
& 3w - 12 = 3w - 12 \\
& \mathbf{-12 = -12} \quad \text{\textbf{TRUE statement!}} \\
& \implies \mathbf{\text{Identity (All Real Numbers, } \mathbb{R}\text{)}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Practice\ 4:}\quad & -4x + 4x - 16 = -11 \\
& \mathbf{-16 = -11} \quad \text{\textbf{FALSE statement!}} \\
& \implies \mathbf{\text{Contradiction (No Solution, } \emptyset\text{)}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Distribute fully in #3:** $3(w-9) = 3w - 27$. Then $-27 + 15 = -12$. The left and right sides match identically!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Practice #3, distributing 3 gives $3w - 12 = 3w - 12$. That is an Identity: All Real Numbers.

[TA Sora] In Practice #4, $-4x + 4x$ cancels to 0, leaving $-16 = -11$, which is false! Contradiction: No Solution.

### [Slide 5] In-Class Practice #5 & #6
*Unit 1 • Lecture 12 • Section 1.6 Extra Practice #5 & #6 (Workbook p. 19)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve and classify (Workbook p. 19)
$$\text{5. } -(-y - 28) = 7(y + 4)$$
$$\text{6. } -2(r + 3) + 7 = 1 - 2r$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Practice\ 5:}\quad & y + 28 = 7y + 28 \\
& 28 - 28 = 7y - y \\
& 0 = 6y \implies \mathbf{y = 0} \quad \mathbf{[Conditional]} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Practice\ 6:}\quad & -2r - 6 + 7 = 1 - 2r \\
& -2r + 1 = 1 - 2r \\
& \mathbf{1 = 1} \quad \text{\textbf{TRUE statement!}} \\
& \implies \mathbf{\text{Identity (All Real Numbers, } \mathbb{R}\text{)}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Practice #5 Check:** $y = 0$ is a UNIQUE real number solution, so Practice #5 is CONDITIONAL!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Practice #5, distributing the negative gives $y + 28 = 7y + 28 \implies 6y = 0 \implies y = 0$. That is Conditional.

[TA Sora] And in Practice #6, $-2r + 1 = 1 - 2r \implies 1 = 1$. True for every value of $r$! That is an Identity: All Real Numbers.
