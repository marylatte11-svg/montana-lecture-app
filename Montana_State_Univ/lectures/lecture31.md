- **General Form:** $\mathbf{f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c}$ where $a \neq 0$.
- **Standard / Vertex Form:** $\mathbf{f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k}$.
- **Graph:** A smooth, symmetric U-shaped curve called a **Parabola**!

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{General Form: } & f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c \quad (a \neq 0) \\

\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note

## Slide 2: Anatomy of a Quadratic Function: Terms & Coefficients

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 31 • Section 3.0 (Workbook p. 60)

### Problem
### Anatomy of General Form (Workbook p. 60)
$$\mathbf{f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c}$$
- **Quadratic Term:** The term with $x^2$ $\rightarrow$ **$ax^2$** (Coefficient is **$a$**).
- **Linear Term:** The term with $x$ $\rightarrow$ **$bx$** (Coefficient is **$b$**).
- **Constant Term:** The numerical term without variables $\rightarrow$ **$c$**.

### Solution
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Term Type} & \textbf{Algebraic Expression} & \textbf{Coefficient} \\
\hline
\text{Quadratic Term} & ax^2 & a \\
\hline
\text{Linear Term} & bx & b \\
\hline
\text{Constant Term} & c & c \\
\hline
\end{array}$$

### Key Note
**Coefficient vs Term:** The term includes the variable ($ax^2$). The coefficient is strictly the number in front ($a$)!

[Prof. Park] In Section 3.0, the authors ask us to identify the quadratic term, linear term, and constant term.

[TA Sora] Make sure to keep the sign with the coefficient: if it says $-5x$, the linear term is $-5x$ and its coefficient is $-5$!

## Slide 3: Section 3.0 Example 1: Terms in f(x) = -x^2 + 3x + 8

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 31 • Section 3.0 Example 1 (Workbook p. 60)

### Problem
### Example 1 (Workbook p. 60)
For the function $\mathbf{f(x) = -x^2 + 3x + 8}$, identify:
- **Quadratic term:** ____________ \quad **Coefficient:** ____________
- **Linear term:** ____________ \quad **Coefficient:** ____________
- **Constant term:** ____________

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\textbf{Quadratic term: } & \mathbf{-x^2} \quad \implies \quad \textbf{Coefficient: } \mathbf{-1} \\[0.5em]
\textbf{Linear term: } & \mathbf{3x} \quad \implies \quad \textbf{Coefficient: } \mathbf{3} \\[0.5em]
\textbf{Constant term: } & \mathbf{8}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**The invisible 1:** When you see $-x^2$, the coefficient is $-1$, NOT $0$ or just a minus sign!

[TA Sora] Linear term is $3x$ with coefficient 3, and the constant is 8. Notice $a = -1 < 0$, which means this parabola opens downward!

## Slide 4: Section 3.0 Example 2: Terms in f(x) = 2x^2 - 7

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 31 • Section 3.0 Example 2 (Workbook p. 60)

### Problem
### Example 2 (Workbook p. 60)
For the function $\mathbf{f(x) = 2x^2 - 7}$, identify:
- **Quadratic term:** ____________ \quad **Coefficient:** ____________
- **Linear term:** ____________ \quad **Coefficient:** ____________
- **Constant term:** ____________

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\textbf{Quadratic term: } & \mathbf{2x^2} \quad \implies \quad \textbf{Coefficient: } \mathbf{2} \\[0.5em]
\textbf{Linear term: } & \mathbf{0x \; (\text{None})} \quad \implies \quad \textbf{Coefficient: } \mathbf{0} \\[0.5em]
\textbf{Constant term: } & \mathbf{-7}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Missing terms have coefficient 0:** Because there is no $x$ term, the linear coefficient is $b = 0$. Do not write 'none'—mathematically, $b = 0$!

## Slide 5: Section 3.0 Example 3: Descending Order First

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 31 • Section 3.0 Example 3 (Workbook p. 60)

### Problem
### Example 3 (Workbook p. 60)
Consider $\mathbf{f(x) = -2 - 3x + \frac{x^2}{3}}$.
- **Put in descending order first:** __________________________
- **Quadratic term & coefficient:** __________________________
- **Linear term & coefficient:** __________________________
- **Constant term:** __________________________

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\textbf{Descending Order: } & \mathbf{f(x) = \frac{1}{3}x^2 - 3x - 2} \\[0.8em]
\textbf{Quadratic term: } & \mathbf{\frac{x^2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}x^2} \quad \implies \quad \textbf{Coefficient: } \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}} \\[0.5em]
\textbf{Linear term: } & \mathbf{-3x} \quad \implies \quad \textbf{Coefficient: } \mathbf{-3} \\[0.5em]
\textbf{Constant term: } & \mathbf{-2}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Rearrange before identifying:** Don't assume the first term is quadratic! Always rearrange from highest exponent to lowest: $x^2$, then $x$, then constant!

### Script
[Prof. Park] In Example 3, the terms are scrambled. Write the $x^2$ term first, then the $x$ term, then the constant.

[TA Sora] $\frac{x^2}{3} - 3x - 2$. The quadratic coefficient is $\frac{1}{3}$, the linear coefficient is $-3$, and the constant is $-2$!

## Slide 6: Section 3.0 Example 4: Linear vs Quadratic

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 31 • Section 3.0 Example 4 (Workbook p. 60)

### Problem
### Example 4 (Workbook p. 60)
What type of function is $\mathbf{f(x) = 4x + 7}$?
- **Quadratic term & coefficient:** __________________________
- **Linear term & coefficient:** __________________________
- **Constant term:** __________________________
- Is this function quadratic?

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\textbf{Quadratic term: } & \mathbf{0x^2 \; (\text{None})} \quad \implies \quad \textbf{Coefficient: } \mathbf{0} \\[0.5em]
\textbf{Linear term: } & \mathbf{4x} \quad \implies \quad \textbf{Coefficient: } \mathbf{4} \\[0.5em]
\textbf{Constant term: } & \mathbf{7} \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Function Type: }} & \mathbf{\text{LINEAR FUNCTION, NOT Quadratic!}}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**A quadratic function MUST have an x^2 term:** Since $a = 0$, the degree is 1, so this is a straight line, not a parabola!

### Script
[Prof. Park] Example 4 tests our definition. $f(x) = 4x + 7$ has no $x^2$ term at all.

[TA Sora] It is a linear function with slope 4 and $y$-intercept 7. Quadratics strictly require degree 2!

## Slide 7: Parabola Direction: Opening Upward vs Opening Downward

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 31 • Section 3.0 Direction Rules

### Problem
### Parabola Orientation Rules
The sign of the leading coefficient $a$ determines which way the parabola opens:
- **$a > 0$ (Positive):** Parabola opens **UPWARD** $\cup$.
  - The vertex is the **lowest point (Minimum)**.
- **$a < 0$ (Negative):** Parabola opens **DOWNWARD** $\cap$.
  - The vertex is the **highest point (Maximum)**.

### Solution
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Sign of } a & \textbf{Shape} & \textbf{Orientation} & \textbf{Vertex Type} \\
\hline
a > 0 & \cup & \text{Opens UP} & \textbf{Minimum (Lowest Point)} \\
\hline
a < 0 & \cap & \text{Opens DOWN} & \textbf{Maximum (Highest Point)} \\
\hline
\end{array}$$

### Key Note
**Happy face vs Sad face:** Positive $a > 0$ makes a smile (minimum valley). Negative $a < 0$ makes a frown (maximum peak)!

### Script
[Prof. Park] Look at the two parabolas on your screen. The blue curve opens upward because $a > 0$, while the pink curve opens downward because $a < 0$.

[TA Sora] This is so visual: positive means a minimum valley, negative means a maximum peak!
