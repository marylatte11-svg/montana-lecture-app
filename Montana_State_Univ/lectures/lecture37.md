## Slide 1: Square Root Property with Binomial Squares: (x - h)^2 = k

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 37 • Section 3.2 (Workbook p. 69)

### Problem
### The Generalized Square Root Property (Workbook p. 69)
When a binomial squared equals a constant:
$$\mathbf{(x - h)^2 = k}$$
- Take the square root of both sides:
  $$\mathbf{x - h = \pm \sqrt{k}}$$
- Add $h$ to both sides to isolate $x$:
  $$\mathbf{x = h \pm \sqrt{k}}$$
- This produces two solutions: $x_1 = h + \sqrt{k}$ and $x_2 = h - \sqrt{k}$.

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Ensure the squared term } (x - h)^2 \text{ is isolated.} \\
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Apply } \pm \sqrt{k}: \quad x - h = \pm \sqrt{k}. \\
\text{Step 3: } & \text{Add } h \text{ in FRONT of the } \pm: \quad x = h \pm \sqrt{k}. \\
\text{Step 4: } & \text{Calculate both numerical solutions if } k \text{ is a perfect square.}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Put h in front of the $\pm$ sign:** Writing $x = h \pm \sqrt{k}$ prevents you from accidentally putting $h$ inside the radical!

### Script
[Prof. Park] In Lecture 37, we solve equations where the entire left side is a binomial squared: $(x - h)^2 = k$.

[TA Sora] Take the square root of both sides, drop the exponent 2, and add $h$ to both sides! It's that clean.

## Slide 2: Section 3.2 Example 5: Solving (x - 3)^2 = 25

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 37 • Section 3.2 Example 5 (Workbook p. 69)

### Problem
### Example 5 (Workbook p. 69)
Solve the equation for $x$:
$$\mathbf{(x - 3)^2 = 25}$$
- Apply the Square Root Property.
- Separate into two arithmetic cases and simplify.

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
(x - 3)^2 & = 25 \\[0.5em]
x - 3 & = \pm \sqrt{25} \\[0.5em]
x - 3 & = \pm 5 \\[0.5em]
x & = 3 \pm 5 \\[0.8em]
\text{Case 1: } & x = 3 + 5 = \mathbf{8} \\
\text{Case 2: } & x = 3 - 5 = \mathbf{-2} \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Solutions: }} & \mathbf{x = 8 \quad \text{and} \quad x = -2}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Do not FOIL out $(x - 3)^2$!** Expanding $(x - 3)^2 = x^2 - 6x + 9 = 25$ wastes time and invites arithmetic errors. Take the square root directly!

### Script
[Prof. Park] In Example 5, take square roots immediately: $x - 3 = \pm 5$. Adding 3 gives $3 \pm 5$.

[TA Sora] $3 + 5 = 8$, and $3 - 5 = -2$. Two integer solutions!

## Slide 3: Section 3.2 Example 6: Intercepts & Vertex for f(x) = (x + 2)^2 - 9

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 37 • Section 3.2 Example 6 (Workbook p. 69)

### Problem
### Example 6 (Workbook p. 69)
Find the intercepts and vertex for the function:
$$\mathbf{f(x) = (x + 2)^2 - 9}$$
- **A. Find the $x$-intercept(s):** __________________________
- **B. Find the $y$-intercept:** __________________________
- **C. Find the vertex:** __________________________

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\textbf{A. x-intercepts: } & \text{Set } f(x) = 0: (x + 2)^2 - 9 = 0 \implies (x + 2)^2 = 9 \\
& x + 2 = \pm \sqrt{9} = \pm 3 \\
& x = -2 \pm 3 \implies x = -2 + 3 = \mathbf{1}, \quad x = -2 - 3 = \mathbf{-5} \\
& \implies \mathbf{(1, 0) \quad \text{and} \quad (-5, 0)} \\[0.8em]
\textbf{B. y-intercept: } & f(0) = (0 + 2)^2 - 9 = 4 - 9 = \mathbf{-5} \implies \mathbf{(0, -5)} \\[0.8em]
\textbf{C. Vertex: } & \text{From vertex form: } \mathbf{(-2, -9)}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Check vertex form:** Vertex is $(-2, -9)$. Notice the $x$-intercepts $1$ and $-5$ are spaced $\pm 3$ units symmetrically around $x = -2$!

### Script
[Prof. Park] In Example 6, setting $(x + 2)^2 - 9 = 0$ gives $x + 2 = \pm 3$.

[TA Sora] So $x = -2 \pm 3$, which gives $(1, 0)$ and $(-5, 0)$. And the vertex is $(-2, -9)$!

## Slide 4: Section 3.2 Example 7: Solving 2(x - 4)^2 = 32

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 37 • Section 3.2 Example 7 (Workbook p. 70)

### Problem
### Example 7 (Workbook p. 70)
Solve the equation for $x$:
$$\mathbf{2(x - 4)^2 = 32}$$
- Divide by 2 first to isolate $(x - 4)^2$.
- Apply the Square Root Property.
- Solve for both values of $x$.

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
2(x - 4)^2 & = 32 \\[0.5em]
(x - 4)^2 & = 16 \quad (\text{divide by } 2) \\[0.5em]
x - 4 & = \pm \sqrt{16} \\[0.5em]
x - 4 & = \pm 4 \\[0.5em]
x & = 4 \pm 4 \\[0.8em]
\text{Case 1: } & x = 4 + 4 = \mathbf{8} \\
\text{Case 2: } & x = 4 - 4 = \mathbf{0} \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Solutions: }} & \mathbf{x = 8 \quad \text{and} \quad x = 0}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Do not distribute 2 into parentheses:** $(x - 4)$ is protected by the exponent 2! You CANNOT distribute 2 into $(x - 4)$! Divide by 2 instead!

### Script
[Prof. Park] In Example 7, divide both sides by 2 first to get $(x - 4)^2 = 16$.

[TA Sora] Taking square roots gives $x - 4 = \pm 4 \implies x = 4 \pm 4$, so $x = 8$ or $x = 0$!

## Slide 5: Section 3.2 Example 8: Non-Perfect Squares and Radicals

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 37 • Section 3.2 Example 8 (Workbook p. 70)

### Problem
### Example 8 (Workbook p. 70)
Solve the equation for $x$:
$$\mathbf{(x - 1)^2 = 12}$$
- Apply the Square Root Property.
- Simplify the radical $\sqrt{12} = \sqrt{4 \cdot 3} = 2\sqrt{3}$.
- Express the exact answers in simplified radical form.

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
(x - 1)^2 & = 12 \\[0.5em]
x - 1 & = \pm \sqrt{12} \\[0.5em]
x - 1 & = \pm \sqrt{4 \cdot 3} = \pm 2\sqrt{3} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{x} & = \mathbf{1 \pm 2\sqrt{3}} \\[0.8em]
\text{Two exact roots: } & \mathbf{x = 1 + 2\sqrt{3}} \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{x = 1 - 2\sqrt{3}}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Do not combine 1 and 2:** $1 \pm 2\sqrt{3}$ CANNOT be simplified to $3\sqrt{3}$! $1$ is a rational number and $2\sqrt{3}$ is irrational; they are not like terms!

### Script
[Prof. Park] In Example 8, 12 is not a perfect square. We simplify $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$.

[TA Sora] Adding 1 gives $1 \pm 2\sqrt{3}$. Remember: do NOT add 1 and 2 together! $1$ and $2\sqrt{3}$ are not like terms!

## Slide 6: Exact Radical Form vs Decimal Approximations

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 37 • Section 3.2 Approximations

### Problem
### Working with Irrational Roots
For $x = 1 \pm 2\sqrt{3}$:
- Compute the decimal approximations using $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$.
- Verify where these points lie on the $x$-axis of the coordinate plane.

### Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{3} & \approx 1.732 \\[0.5em]
2\sqrt{3} & \approx 2(1.732) = 3.464 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{x_1} & = 1 + 2\sqrt{3} \approx 1 + 3.464 = \mathbf{4.46} \implies \mathbf{(4.46, 0)} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{x_2} & = 1 - 2\sqrt{3} \approx 1 - 3.464 = \mathbf{-2.46} \implies \mathbf{(-2.46, 0)}
\end{aligned}$$

### Key Note
**Exam instruction awareness:** If the exam asks for 'exact form', write $1 \pm 2\sqrt{3}$. If it asks for 'round to nearest tenth', write $4.5$ and $-2.5$!

### Script
[Prof. Park] Exact form is $1 \pm 2\sqrt{3}$. On a graph, that corresponds to approximately $4.46$ and $-2.46$.

[TA Sora] Notice on the coordinate grid how the parabola crosses the $x$-axis right around $-2.5$ and $4.5$!

## Slide 7: Sora's Checklist for the Square Root Property

**Subtitle:** Unit 3 • Lecture 37 • Section 3.2 Checklist

### Problem
### When to Use the Square Root Property
- **Use SRP when:**
  - Equation has an isolated square: $x^2 = k$ or $(x - h)^2 = k$.
  - There is NO separate linear $x$ term outside the parentheses.
- **Do NOT use SRP directly if:**
  - Equation has both $x^2$ and $x$ separated: e.g. $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$.
  *(For that, we use Factoring or Completing the Square!)*

### Solution
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|c|} 
\hline
\textbf{Equation} & \textbf{Use SRP?} & \textbf{Reason} \\
\hline
(x - 5)^2 = 16 & \textbf{YES} & \text{Isolated binomial square} \\
\hline
3x^2 = 75 & \textbf{YES} & \text{Isolate } x^2 = 25 \\
\hline
x^2 - 6x + 8 = 0 & \textbf{NO} & \text{Has linear term } -6x \; (\text{use Factoring}) \\
\hline
\end{array}$$

### Key Note
**Pattern recognition:** Recognizing which tool fits which equation is what makes math easy!

### Script
[Prof. Park] This table teaches pattern recognition. If you see a complete square, use the Square Root Property!

[TA Sora] If you see a full trinomial with a middle term, use Factoring—which is our next lecture!
