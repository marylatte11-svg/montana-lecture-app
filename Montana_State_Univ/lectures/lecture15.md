### [Slide 1] Section 1.9: Solving Linear Inequalities & Interval Notation
*Unit 1 • Lecture 15 • Section 1.9 (Workbook p. 25)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### The Golden Rule of Inequalities (Workbook p. 25-26)
$$\text{When you MULTIPLY or DIVIDE both sides by a NEGATIVE number,}$$ $$\text{you MUST REVERSE (FLIP) the inequality symbol!}$$
- Example: $-2x < 6 \implies \frac{-2x}{-2} > \frac{6}{-2} \implies \mathbf{x > -3}$.
- **Interval Notation:** Parentheses $( \ )$ for strict ($<, >$), brackets $[ \ ]$ for inclusive ($\le, \ge$).
- **Infinities:** $\infty$ and $-\infty$ **ALWAYS take parentheses**.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Strict: } & x > a \implies (a, \infty) \quad \text{and} \quad x < a \implies (-\infty, a) \\
\text{Inclusive: } & x \ge a \implies [a, \infty) \quad \text{and} \quad x \le a \implies (-\infty, a]
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Absolute Rule:** If you don't flip the sign when dividing by a negative, your entire solution set is reversed and wrong!

[TA Sora] The #1 rule: Whenever you multiply or divide by a negative number, FLIP THE INEQUALITY SIGN! Let's see Example 1 on Slide 2.

### [Slide 2] Example 1A & 1B: Basic Interval Notation
*Unit 1 • Lecture 15 • Section 1.9 Example 1A & 1B (Workbook p. 25)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Represent each on a number line graph and convert to interval notation (Workbook p. 25)
$$\text{A. } x > 5$$
$$\text{B. } x < -6$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & \text{Open circle at } 5, \text{ shaded to the right towards } +\infty \\
& \mathbf{(5, \infty)} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & \text{Open circle at } -6, \text{ shaded to the left towards } -\infty \\
& \mathbf{(-\infty, -6)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Order Matters:** In interval notation, always list numbers from LEFT to RIGHT as they appear on the number line! $(-\infty, -6)$, NOT $(-6, -\infty)$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1A: $x > 5$. That means all numbers strictly greater than 5, stretching to infinity: $(5, \infty)$.

[TA Sora] And in 1B: $x < -6$. That starts at negative infinity and stops at $-6$: $(-\infty, -6)$.

### [Slide 3] Example 1C & 1D: Variable on the Right Side
*Unit 1 • Lecture 15 • Section 1.9 Example 1C & 1D (Workbook p. 25)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Represent on number line and convert to interval notation (Workbook p. 25)
$$\text{C. } -3 \le x$$
$$\text{D. } 12 \ge x$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & \text{Step 1: Rewrite with } x \text{ on the left side (keep arrow pointing at } -3\text{):} \\
& -3 \le x \iff \mathbf{x \ge -3} \\
& \text{Solid bracket at } -3, \text{ shade right: } \mathbf{[-3, \infty)} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & \text{Step 1: Rewrite with } x \text{ on the left side (arrow points at } x\text{):} \\
& 12 \ge x \iff \mathbf{x \le 12} \\
& \text{Solid bracket at } 12, \text{ shade left: } \mathbf{(-\infty, 12]}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Reading Direction Trap:** When $x$ is on the right, read from the variable! $-3 \le x$ means '$x$ is greater than or equal to $-3$'! Always rewrite with $x$ on the left first!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1C: $-3 \le x$. Sora, what should students do when the variable is on the right?

[TA Sora] Flip the whole statement so $x$ comes first! Keep the small point pointing at $-3$: $x \ge -3$. That means $[-3, \infty)$!

[Prof. Park] In 1D: $12 \ge x$ means $x \le 12$. That is $(-\infty, 12]$.

### [Slide 4] Example 1E & 1F: Double (Bounded) Inequalities
*Unit 1 • Lecture 15 • Section 1.9 Example 1E & 1F (Workbook p. 25)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Represent on number line and convert to interval notation (Workbook p. 25)
$$\text{E. } -3 < x < 1$$
$$\text{F. } 2 \le x < 3$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ E:}\quad & x \text{ is trapped strictly between } -3 \text{ and } 1. \\
& \text{Open circles at both ends: } \mathbf{(-3, 1)} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ F:}\quad & x \text{ is greater than or equal to } 2, \text{ but strictly less than } 3. \\
& \text{Solid bracket at } 2, \text{ parenthesis at } 3: \mathbf{[2, 3)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Mixed Endpoints:** In 1F, $2$ is included (bracket $[$), but $3$ is strict (parenthesis $)$). Never write $[2, 3]$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1E, $x$ is trapped between $-3$ and $1$. Both are strict, so we write $(-3, 1)$.

[TA Sora] In 1F, $x$ includes 2, but does not include 3. Bracket at 2, parenthesis at 3: $[2, 3)$!

### [Slide 5] Example 1G & 1H: Disjoint Compound Inequalities (Union)
*Unit 1 • Lecture 15 • Section 1.9 Example 1G & 1H (Workbook p. 25)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Represent on number line and convert to interval notation (Workbook p. 25)
$$\text{G. } x < -7 \text{ or } x \ge 0$$
$$\text{H. } x \le 2 \text{ or } x > 5$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ G:}\quad & \text{Two separate arrows pointing outward:} \\
& x < -7 \implies (-\infty, -7) \\
& x \ge 0 \implies [0, \infty) \\
& \text{Combine with union symbol } \cup: \mathbf{(-\infty, -7) \cup [0, \infty)} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ H:}\quad & x \le 2 \implies (-\infty, 2] \\
& x > 5 \implies (5, \infty) \\
& \text{Combine with union: } \mathbf{(-\infty, 2] \cup (5, \infty)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Union Symbol:** Use $\cup$ (union) to glue two disjoint intervals together!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] When an inequality says 'or', the solutions point in opposite directions.

[TA Sora] In 1G, $(-\infty, -7)$ combined with $[0, \infty)$ using the union symbol $\cup$: $(-\infty, -7) \cup [0, \infty)$. Same in 1H: $(-\infty, 2] \cup (5, \infty)$!

### [Slide 6] Example 2A & 2B: Solving Linear Inequalities
*Unit 1 • Lecture 15 • Section 1.9 Example 2A & 2B (Workbook p. 26)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve each inequality. Write final answer in interval notation (Workbook p. 26)
$$\text{A. } -6 + 3x \le -12 + 5x$$
$$\text{B. } 4(5x - 7) > 10 + 5x$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & 3x - 5x \le -12 + 6 \\
& -2x \le -6 \\
& \text{Divide by } -2 \text{ and } \textbf{FLIP the inequality sign!} \\
& x \ge \frac{-6}{-2} \implies x \ge 3 \implies \mathbf{[3, \infty)} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & 20x - 28 > 10 + 5x \\
& 20x - 5x > 10 + 28 \\
& 15x > 38 \\
& x > \frac{38}{15} \implies \mathbf{\left(\frac{38}{15}, \infty\right)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**The Flip in 2A:** Dividing $-2x \le -6$ by $-2$ FLIPS the sign to $\ge$: $x \ge 3$, meaning $[3, \infty)$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 2A, subtracting $5x$ gives $-2x \le -6$. Dividing by $-2$ flips $\le$ to $\ge$: $x \ge 3 \implies [3, \infty)$.

[TA Sora] In 2B, distribute first: $20x - 28 > 10 + 5x \implies 15x > 38 \implies x > \frac{38}{15} \implies (\frac{38}{15}, \infty)$.

### [Slide 7] Example 2C & 2D: Inequalities with Fractions & Negatives
*Unit 1 • Lecture 15 • Section 1.9 Example 2C & 2D (Workbook p. 26)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve each inequality and write in interval notation (Workbook p. 26)
$$\text{C. } \frac{4}{5}y + 5 < 6$$
$$\text{D. } 2 \le \frac{x}{-8} - 3$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & \text{Subtract } 5: \quad \frac{4}{5}y < 1 \\
& \text{Multiply by } \frac{5}{4}: \quad y < \frac{5}{4} \implies \mathbf{\left(-\infty, \frac{5}{4}\right)} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & \text{Add } 3 \text{ to both sides:} \quad 5 \le \frac{x}{-8} \\
& \text{Multiply by } -8 \text{ and } \textbf{FLIP the inequality sign!} \\
& -8 \cdot 5 \ge x \implies -40 \ge x \iff x \le -40 \\
& \mathbf{(-\infty, -40]}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Multiplying by Negative Denominator:** In 2D, multiplying by $-8$ flips $\le$ to $\ge$! $-40 \ge x$ means $x \le -40$, which is $(-\infty, -40]$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 2C, subtracting 5 gives $\frac{4}{5}y < 1 \implies y < \frac{5}{4} \implies (-\infty, \frac{5}{4})$.

[TA Sora] And in 2D, adding 3 gives $5 \le \frac{x}{-8}$. Multiplying by $-8$ flips the sign: $-40 \ge x \implies x \le -40 \implies (-\infty, -40]$!

### [Slide 8] Example 2E & 2F: Binomial Numerator Inequality Comparison
*Unit 1 • Lecture 15 • Section 1.9 Example 2E & 2F (Workbook p. 26)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve both and compare the effect of negative denominator (Workbook p. 26)
$$\text{E. } \frac{x - 30}{-4} \ge 50$$
$$\text{F. } \frac{x - 30}{4} > 50$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ E:}\quad & \text{Multiply by } -4 \text{ and } \textbf{FLIP} \ge \text{ to } \le: \\
& x - 30 \le 50 \cdot (-4) \\
& x - 30 \le -200 \implies x \le -170 \implies \mathbf{(-\infty, -170]} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ F:}\quad & \text{Multiply by positive } 4 \text{ (sign does } \textbf{NOT} \text{ flip!):} \\
& x - 30 > 50 \cdot 4 \\
& x - 30 > 200 \implies x > 230 \implies \mathbf{(230, \infty)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Compare the Two:** In 2E, the $-4$ in the denominator flips $\ge$ to $\le$, pointing left! In 2F, the positive $4$ keeps $>$ pointing right! Two totally different answers!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Compare Examples 2E and 2F side-by-side. In 2E, multiplying by $-4$ flips the inequality to $x - 30 \le -200 \implies x \le -170 \implies (-\infty, -170]$.

[TA Sora] But in 2F, 4 is positive, so the sign stays $>$: $x - 30 > 200 \implies x > 230 \implies (230, \infty)$. What a perfect demonstration of the negative multiplication rule!
