### [Slide 1] Library of Basic Parent Functions & Interval Notation
*Unit 2 • Lecture 17 • Section 2.0 (Workbook p. 31)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Basic Equations & Domain/Range (Workbook p. 31)
In Section 2.0, we explore the 7 fundamental shapes of algebra:
1. Constant: $y = c$
2. Linear: $y = x$
3. Quadratic: $y = x^2$
4. Square Root: $y = \sqrt{x}$
5. Absolute Value: $y = \lvert x \rvert$
6. Cubic: $y = x^3$
7. Cube Root: $y = \sqrt[3]{x}$

State the **Domain** ($x$-values) and **Range** ($y$-values) in **interval notation**.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Domain: } & \text{Set of all possible input } x\text{-values (left-to-right)} \\
\text{Range: } & \text{Set of all possible output } y\text{-values (bottom-to-top)} \\
\text{Interval Notation: } & [\text{inclusive}], \quad (\text{exclusive or } \pm\infty)
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Interval bounds order:** Always write intervals from least to greatest: $(\text{smaller}, \text{larger})$. Never write $(5, -2)$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Lecture 17, we build the fundamental library of function shapes that you will use in all future math courses.

[TA Sora] We will graph each equation by plotting points, and state its domain and range in interval notation!

### [Slide 2] Basic Equation A: Constant Function y = 3
*Unit 2 • Lecture 17 • Section 2.0 A (Workbook p. 31)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Equation A: $y = 3$ (Workbook p. 31)
- Create a table of values for $x = -2, 0, 2$.
- Graph the equation on the Cartesian coordinate plane.
- State the **Domain** and **Range** in interval notation.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Table of Values: } & (-2, 3), \; (0, 3), \; (2, 3) \\[0.5em]
\text{Graph Shape: } & \mathbf{\text{Horizontal Line at } y = 3} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Domain: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)} \quad (x \text{ can be any real number}) \\
\mathbf{\text{Range: }} & \mathbf{\{3\}} \quad (y \text{ is strictly locked at } 3)
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Range of a constant:** The range contains only a single number, so we write $\{3\}$ or $[3, 3]$, not $(-\infty, \infty)$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Notice that no matter what $x$ you pick, $y$ remains 3. That produces a perfectly flat, horizontal line.

[TA Sora] Domain is $(-\infty, \infty)$ because the line extends left and right forever. But range is just the single value $\{3\}$!

### [Slide 3] Basic Equation B: Linear Identity y = x
*Unit 2 • Lecture 17 • Section 2.0 B (Workbook p. 31)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Equation B: $y = x$ (Workbook p. 31)
- Create a table of values for $x = -2, 0, 2$.
- Graph the equation on the Cartesian coordinate plane.
- State the **Domain** and **Range** in interval notation.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Table of Values: } & (-2, -2), \; (0, 0), \; (2, 2) \\[0.5em]
\text{Graph Shape: } & \mathbf{\text{Diagonal Line passing through the origin at } 45^\circ} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Domain: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)} \\
\mathbf{\text{Range: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Identity relationship:** Every output equals its input: $x = y$. It passes directly through $(0, 0)$ with slope $m = 1$.

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In $y = x$, the input and output are identical. $(-2, -2)$, $(0,0)$, $(2,2)$.

[TA Sora] It slants upward at a perfect 45-degree angle. Both domain and range are all real numbers $(-\infty, \infty)$!

### [Slide 4] Basic Equation C: Quadratic Parabola y = x^2
*Unit 2 • Lecture 17 • Section 2.0 C (Workbook p. 31)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Equation C: $y = x^2$ (Workbook p. 31)
- Create a table of values for $x = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2$.
- Graph the equation on the Cartesian coordinate plane.
- State the **Domain** and **Range** in interval notation.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Table of Values: } & (-2, 4), \; (-1, 1), \; (0, 0), \; (1, 1), \; (2, 4) \\[0.5em]
\text{Graph Shape: } & \mathbf{\text{U-shaped Curve (Parabola) with vertex at } (0, 0)} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Domain: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)} \\
\mathbf{\text{Range: }} & \mathbf{[0, \infty)} \quad (\text{since } x^2 \ge 0 \text{ for all real } x)
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Squaring negatives:** $(-2)^2 = +4$, NOT $-4$! Because outputs are never negative, the range starts at $0$ with a bracket $[0, \infty)$.

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] When we square a real number, the result is never negative. Look at the U-shape called a parabola.

[TA Sora] Notice the lowest point is the vertex at $(0,0)$. The range is $[0, \infty)$ with a bracket on 0 because 0 is included!

### [Slide 5] Basic Equation D: Square Root Curve y = sqrt(x)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 17 • Section 2.0 D (Workbook p. 31)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Equation D: $y = \sqrt{x}$ (Workbook p. 31)
- Create a table of values for $x = 0, 1, 4, 9$.
- Graph the equation on the Cartesian coordinate plane.
- State the **Domain** and **Range** in interval notation.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Table of Values: } & (0, 0), \; (1, 1), \; (4, 2), \; (9, 3) \\[0.5em]
\text{Graph Shape: } & \mathbf{\text{Half-sideways curve starting at } (0, 0)} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Domain: }} & \mathbf{[0, \infty)} \quad (\text{cannot take square root of negative numbers}) \\
\mathbf{\text{Range: }} & \mathbf{[0, \infty)} \quad (\text{principal square root is non-negative})
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Negative radicands:** In the real number system, $\sqrt{-4}$ is not a real number. Therefore $x$ cannot be negative!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In $y = \sqrt{x}$, we cannot plug in negative numbers in real algebra. So the graph starts at the origin $(0, 0)$.

[TA Sora] Domain is $[0, \infty)$ and Range is $[0, \infty)$! The curve rises gradually to the right.

### [Slide 7] Basic Equation F: Cubic Function y = x^3
*Unit 2 • Lecture 17 • Section 2.0 F (Workbook p. 32)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Equation F: $y = x^3$ (Workbook p. 32)
- Create a table of values for $x = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2$.
- Graph the equation on the Cartesian coordinate plane.
- State the **Domain** and **Range** in interval notation.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Table of Values: } & (-2, -8), \; (-1, -1), \; (0, 0), \; (1, 1), \; (2, 8) \\[0.5em]
\text{Graph Shape: } & \mathbf{\text{S-shaped Snake Curve passing through the origin}} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Domain: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)} \\
\mathbf{\text{Range: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Odd powers preserve signs:** $(-2)^3 = -8$, while $(+2)^3 = +8$. Unlike $x^2$, cubic graphs go down to $-\infty$ on the left and up to $+\infty$ on the right!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In $y = x^3$, odd powers keep the sign of the base. Negative numbers produce negative cubes.

[TA Sora] This makes an S-curve that passes through the origin. Both domain and range are all real numbers $(-\infty, \infty)$!

### [Slide 8] Basic Equation G: Cube Root y = cbrt(x)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 17 • Section 2.0 G (Workbook p. 32)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Equation G: $y = \sqrt[3]{x}$ (Workbook p. 32)
- Create a table of values for $x = -8, -1, 0, 1, 8$.
- Graph the equation on the Cartesian coordinate plane.
- State the **Domain** and **Range** in interval notation.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Table of Values: } & (-8, -2), \; (-1, -1), \; (0, 0), \; (1, 1), \; (8, 2) \\[0.5em]
\text{Graph Shape: } & \mathbf{\text{Sideways S-shaped Curve extending horizontally}} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Domain: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)} \quad (\text{odd roots are defined for negative numbers!}) \\
\mathbf{\text{Range: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Odd roots CAN take negative inputs:** Unlike $\sqrt{-8}$ which is not real, $\sqrt[3]{-8} = -2$ is completely valid and real!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Notice the critical distinction between even roots and odd roots: you can take the cube root of negative 8, which is negative 2!

[TA Sora] Yes! So the domain of $\sqrt[3]{x}$ is all real numbers $(-\infty, \infty)$, unlike square root which stopped at 0.
