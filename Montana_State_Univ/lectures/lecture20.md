### [Slide 1] The Slope of a Line: Rise Over Run & Formula
*Unit 2 • Lecture 20 • Section 2.2 (Workbook p. 38)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Section 2.2: Slope of a Line (Workbook p. 38)
Slope measures the **steepness and direction** of a straight line:
$$\mathbf{\text{Slope } = m = \frac{\text{Rise}}{\text{Run}} = \frac{\text{Change in } y}{\text{Change in } x} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}}$$
- **Positive Slope ($m > 0$):** Line rises from left to right.
- **Negative Slope ($m < 0$):** Line falls from left to right.
- **Zero Slope ($m = 0$):** Horizontal line.
- **Undefined Slope:** Vertical line (division by zero).

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Formula: } & m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \\[0.5em]
\text{Numerator: } & \Delta y = y_2 - y_1 \quad (\text{vertical rise/fall}) \\
\text{Denominator: } & \Delta x = x_2 - x_1 \quad (\text{horizontal run right})
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Y over X:** Slope is ALWAYS change in $y$ over change in $x$. Never flip it to $\frac{x_2 - x_1}{y_2 - y_1}$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Lecture 20! Today we master Slope: the single most important concept in developmental algebra.

[TA Sora] Remember: Rise over Run! $y$ is the elevator on top, $x$ is the sidewalk on the bottom!

### [Slide 2] Section 2.2 Example 1A: Slope between (2, 3) and (5, 7)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 20 • Section 2.2 Example 1A (Workbook p. 38)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 1A (Workbook p. 38)
Find the slope of the line that passes through the two given points:
$$\mathbf{(2, 3) \quad \text{and} \quad (5, 7)}$$
- Label $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$.
- Apply the slope formula and simplify.
- State the rise and run.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Let } & (x_1, y_1) = (2, 3) \quad \text{and} \quad (x_2, y_2) = (5, 7) \\[0.5em]
m & = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{7 - 3}{5 - 2} \\[0.5em]
& = \frac{4}{3} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m: } & \mathbf{\frac{4}{3}} \quad (\text{Rise } = +4, \; \text{Run } = +3)
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Order consistency:** If you start with $y_2$ in the numerator, you MUST start with $x_2$ in the denominator!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 1A, we compute $\frac{7 - 3}{5 - 2} = \frac{4}{3}$.

[TA Sora] Rise is 4, Run is 3! On the coordinate plane, the gold slope triangle shows exactly 4 units up and 3 units right!

### [Slide 3] Section 2.2 Example 1B: Slope between (3, -4) and (-2, -8)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 20 • Section 2.2 Example 1B (Workbook p. 38)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 1B (Workbook p. 38)
Find the slope of the line that passes through the two given points:
$$\mathbf{(3, -4) \quad \text{and} \quad (-2, -8)}$$
- Watch the double negatives carefully when subtracting!
- Simplify the fraction to lowest terms.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Let } & (x_1, y_1) = (3, -4) \quad \text{and} \quad (x_2, y_2) = (-2, -8) \\[0.5em]
m & = \frac{-8 - (-4)}{-2 - 3} \\[0.5em]
& = \frac{-8 + 4}{-5} = \frac{-4}{-5} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m: } & \mathbf{\frac{4}{5}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Double negative sign trap:** $-8 - (-4) = -8 + 4 = -4$. And $\frac{-4}{-5} = +\frac{4}{5}$. A negative divided by a negative is positive!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 1B, double negatives appear in both numerator and denominator.

[TA Sora] $-8 - (-4)$ becomes $-4$, and $-2 - 3$ becomes $-5$. Negative divided by negative is positive $\frac{4}{5}$!

### [Slide 4] Section 2.2 Example 1C: Slope between (3, 7) and (3, -10)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 20 • Section 2.2 Example 1C (Workbook p. 38)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 1C (Workbook p. 38)
Find the slope of the line that passes through the two given points:
$$\mathbf{(3, 7) \quad \text{and} \quad (3, -10)}$$
- What happens to the denominator $x_2 - x_1$?
- State the value of the slope.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
m & = \frac{-10 - 7}{3 - 3} \\[0.5em]
& = \frac{-17}{0} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m: } & \mathbf{\text{Undefined}} \\[0.5em]
\text{Line Type: } & \mathbf{\text{Vertical line } x = 3}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Division by zero is undefined:** When the denominator is $0$, the slope is UNDEFINED. It is NOT zero! (Think of VUX: Vertical = Undefined = X).

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 1C, both $x$-coordinates are 3. The denominator is $3 - 3 = 0$.

[TA Sora] You can never divide by zero! The slope is undefined, which confirms this is a vertical line $x = 3$!

### [Slide 5] Section 2.2 Example 1D: Slope between (-2, -5) and (3, -5)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 20 • Section 2.2 Example 1D (Workbook p. 38)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 1D (Workbook p. 38)
Find the slope of the line that passes through the two given points:
$$\mathbf{(-2, -5) \quad \text{and} \quad (3, -5)}$$
- What happens to the numerator $y_2 - y_1$?
- State the value of the slope.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
m & = \frac{-5 - (-5)}{3 - (-2)} \\[0.5em]
& = \frac{-5 + 5}{3 + 2} = \frac{0}{5} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m: } & \mathbf{0} \\[0.5em]
\text{Line Type: } & \mathbf{\text{Horizontal line } y = -5}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Zero in numerator vs denominator:** $\frac{0}{5} = 0$ (perfectly fine, zero slope). $\frac{5}{0} = \text{Undefined}$ (division by zero error)!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 1D, the $y$-coordinates are both $-5$. The numerator is $-5 - (-5) = 0$.

[TA Sora] Zero divided by 5 is 0! A slope of 0 means a perfectly flat horizontal line $y = -5$ (HOY)!

### [Slide 6] Section 2.2 Example 1E: Fraction Coordinates
*Unit 2 • Lecture 20 • Section 2.2 Example 1E (Workbook p. 38)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 1E: Fraction Coordinates (Workbook p. 38)
Find the slope of the line passing through:
$$\mathbf{\left(\frac{4}{3}, \frac{1}{3}\right) \quad \text{and} \quad \left(-\frac{3}{5}, \frac{7}{5}\right)}$$
- Subtract the $y$-coordinates using a common denominator.
- Subtract the $x$-coordinates using a common denominator.
- Simplify the complex fraction.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta y & = \frac{7}{5} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{21}{15} - \frac{5}{15} = \frac{16}{15} \\[0.6em]
\Delta x & = -\frac{3}{5} - \frac{4}{3} = -\frac{9}{15} - \frac{20}{15} = -\frac{29}{15} \\[0.6em]
m & = \frac{\frac{16}{15}}{-\frac{29}{15}} = \frac{16}{15} \cdot \left(-\frac{15}{29}\right) = \mathbf{-\frac{16}{29}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Simplifying complex fractions:** Multiply by the reciprocal of the denominator! Notice the LCD 15 cancels out beautifully.

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 1E, we use our Unit 1 fraction skills! Finding LCD 15 in both numerator and denominator makes the 15s cancel.

[TA Sora] Leaving a final clean slope of $-\frac{16}{29}$!

### [Slide 7] Section 2.2 Example 2A: Counting Slope from a Graph
*Unit 2 • Lecture 20 • Section 2.2 Example 2A (Workbook p. 39)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 2A (Workbook p. 39)
Find the slope of the line shown in the graph:
- Identify two grid intersections: $(0, 1)$ and $(3, 3)$.
- Count the vertical rise from the first point to the second.
- Count the horizontal run to the right.
- Write the slope $m = \frac{\text{Rise}}{\text{Run}}$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Point 1: } & (0, 1) \\
\text{Point 2: } & (3, 3) \\[0.5em]
\text{Rise: } & 3 - 1 = \mathbf{+2 \text{ units (UP)}} \\
\text{Run: } & 3 - 0 = \mathbf{+3 \text{ units (RIGHT)}} \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m: } & \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Finding clean grid points:** When reading a slope from a graph, always pick points where the line crosses the grid corners exactly, avoiding fractional estimates!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 2, we find slope directly from the graph by counting grid squares.

[TA Sora] From $(0, 1)$ to $(3, 3)$, count UP 2 squares, then RIGHT 3 squares. The slope is positive $\frac{2}{3}$!

### [Slide 8] Section 2.2 Example 2B: Counting Negative Slope from Graph
*Unit 2 • Lecture 20 • Section 2.2 Example 2B (Workbook p. 39)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 2B (Workbook p. 39)
Find the slope of the line shown in the graph:
- Identify two grid intersections: $(0, 4)$ and $(2, 0)$.
- Determine whether the line is rising or falling from left to right.
- Count the rise and run to calculate $m$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Point 1: } & (0, 4) \\
\text{Point 2: } & (2, 0) \\[0.5em]
\text{Rise: } & 0 - 4 = \mathbf{-4 \text{ units (DOWN 4)}} \\
\text{Run: } & 2 - 0 = \mathbf{+2 \text{ units (RIGHT 2)}} \\[0.8em]
m & = \frac{-4}{2} = \mathbf{-2}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Downhill lines must have negative slope:** If the line goes downward as you read from left to right, your slope MUST be negative!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 2B, the line heads downhill. We drop 4 units and move right 2 units.

[TA Sora] $\frac{-4}{2} = -2$. The negative sign is crucial!
