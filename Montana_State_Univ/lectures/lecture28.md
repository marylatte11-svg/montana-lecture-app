### [Slide 1] Section 2.6: Linear Functions f(x) = mx + b
*Unit 2 • Lecture 28 • Section 2.6 (Workbook p. 51)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Linear Functions (Workbook p. 51)
$$\mathbf{f(x) = mx + b}$$
- $m$ is the **constant rate of change** (slope).
- $(0, b)$ is the **vertical intercept** ($y$-intercept).
- **Domain:** $(-\infty, \infty)$ for all non-vertical linear functions.
- **Range:** $(-\infty, \infty)$ when $m \neq 0$.
  *(If $m = 0$, range is the single number $\{b\}$!)*

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Formula: } & f(x) = mx + b \\
\text{Slope: } & m = \frac{f(x_2) - f(x_1)}{x_2 - x_1} \\
\text{Intercept: } & (0, b)
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Linear function range:** All linear functions with non-zero slope have range $(-\infty, \infty)$. Only constant functions $f(x) = c$ have range $\{c\}$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Section 2.6! Here we unite everything we know about lines and functions into $f(x) = mx + b$.

[TA Sora] $m$ is the constant rate of change. It tells us how rapidly the function increases or decreases!

### [Slide 2] Section 2.6 Example 1: Graphing f(x) = 2x - 4
*Unit 2 • Lecture 28 • Section 2.6 Example 1 (Workbook p. 51)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 1 (Workbook p. 51)
Graph the function $\mathbf{f(x) = 2x - 4}$, and identify the following:
- **Slope:** \underline{\hspace{2cm}}
- **$y$-intercept:** \underline{\hspace{2cm}}
- **$x$-intercept:** \underline{\hspace{2cm}}
- **Domain & Range** (in interval notation)

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m: } & \mathbf{2} = \frac{2}{1} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept: }} & \mathbf{(0, -4)} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{x\text{-intercept: }} & 0 = 2x - 4 \implies 2x = 4 \implies \mathbf{x = 2} \implies \mathbf{(2, 0)} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Domain: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)} \\
\mathbf{\text{Range: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Finding x-intercept in function notation:** Set $f(x) = 0$, NOT $x = 0$! Setting $f(x) = 0$ gives $2x - 4 = 0 \implies x = 2$.

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 1, $f(x) = 2x - 4$. The $y$-intercept is $(0, -4)$ and the slope is $2$.

[TA Sora] Setting $f(x) = 0$ yields $x = 2$. So the $x$-intercept is $(2, 0)$! Both intercepts are plotted on the grid.

### [Slide 3] Section 2.6 Example 2: Graphing g(x) = -x + 4
*Unit 2 • Lecture 28 • Section 2.6 Example 2 (Workbook p. 51)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 2 (Workbook p. 51)
Graph the function $\mathbf{g(x) = -x + 4}$, and identify the following:
- **Slope:** \underline{\hspace{2cm}}
- **$y$-intercept:** \underline{\hspace{2cm}}
- **$x$-intercept:** \underline{\hspace{2cm}}
- **Domain & Range** (in interval notation)

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m: } & \mathbf{-1} = \frac{-1}{1} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept: }} & \mathbf{(0, 4)} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{x\text{-intercept: }} & 0 = -x + 4 \implies x = 4 \implies \mathbf{(4, 0)} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Domain: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)} \\
\mathbf{\text{Range: }} & \mathbf{(-\infty, \infty)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Negative slope:** In $g(x) = -x + 4$, the coefficient of $x$ is $-1$. The line falls at a 45-degree angle from left to right!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 2, the slope is $-1$. It crosses the $y$-axis at $(0, 4)$ and the $x$-axis at $(4, 0)$.

[TA Sora] Notice the symmetry! Both intercepts have a coordinate of 4.

### [Slide 5] Section 2.6 Example 4: Creating Function h(x)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 28 • Section 2.6 Example 4 (Workbook p. 52)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 4 (Workbook p. 52)
Find the equation of the linear function $h(x)$ that has a **slope of $\frac{3}{4}$** and a **$y$-intercept at $(0, -5)$**.
- Express the final answer in function notation $h(x) = mx + b$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Given: } & m = \frac{3}{4}, \quad b = -5 \\[0.5em]
\text{Form: } & h(x) = mx + b \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{h(x)} & = \mathbf{\frac{3}{4}x - 5}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Use function name h(x):** The problem asked for $h(x)$, NOT $y$. Always write $h(x) = \frac{3}{4}x - 5$ to receive full credit!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 4, slope $m = \frac{3}{4}$ and $y$-intercept $(0, -5)$ are given directly.

[TA Sora] We write $h(x) = \frac{3}{4}x - 5$. Look at how nicely it graphs on our coordinate grid!

### [Slide 6] Section 2.6 Example 5: Creating Function k(x)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 28 • Section 2.6 Example 5 (Workbook p. 52)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 5 (Workbook p. 52)
Find the equation of the linear function $k(x)$ that has a **slope of $\frac{1}{5}$** and passes through the point **$(-5, 0)$**.
- Use point-slope form with $k(x)$ replacing $y$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Given: } & m = \frac{1}{5}, \quad (x_1, y_1) = (-5, 0) \\[0.5em]
y - 0 & = \frac{1}{5}(x - (-5)) \\[0.5em]
y & = \frac{1}{5}(x + 5) = \frac{1}{5}x + 1 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{k(x)} & = \mathbf{\frac{1}{5}x + 1}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Replace y with k(x):** In function problems, replace $y$ with $k(x)$ at the very end: $k(x) = \frac{1}{5}x + 1$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 5, $(-5, 0)$ is an $x$-intercept. Distributing $\frac{1}{5}(x + 5)$ gives $k(x) = \frac{1}{5}x + 1$.

[TA Sora] So the $y$-intercept is $(0, 1)$! Both points sit perfectly on our line.

### [Slide 7] Section 2.6 Example 6: Function through (2, 3) and (4, 9)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 28 • Section 2.6 Example 6 (Workbook p. 52)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 6 (Workbook p. 52)
Find the equation of the linear function $f(x)$ that passes through the points **$(2, 3)$** and **$(4, 9)$**.
- Calculate the slope $m$.
- Use point-slope form to find $f(x)$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
m & = \frac{9 - 3}{4 - 2} = \frac{6}{2} = \mathbf{3} \\[0.5em]
y - 3 & = 3(x - 2) = 3x - 6 \\[0.5em]
y & = 3x - 3 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{f(x)} & = \mathbf{3x - 3}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Check both points:** $f(2) = 3(2) - 3 = 3$ (works!). $f(4) = 3(4) - 3 = 9$ (works!). Verification takes only 5 seconds!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 6, the rate of change is $\frac{6}{2} = 3$. Point-slope gives $f(x) = 3x - 3$.

[TA Sora] Both $(2, 3)$ and $(4, 9)$ line up with slope 3!

### [Slide 8] Section 2.6 Example 7: Constant Function through (7, 9) and (-4, 9)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 28 • Section 2.6 Example 7 (Workbook p. 52)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 7 (Workbook p. 52)
Find the equation of the linear function $g(x)$ that contains the points **$(7, 9)$** and **$(-4, 9)$**.
- Notice the shared coordinates!
- State the equation in function notation.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
m & = \frac{9 - 9}{-4 - 7} = \frac{0}{-11} = \mathbf{0} \\[0.5em]
\text{Constant Function: } & g(x) = 0x + 9 \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{g(x)} & = \mathbf{9} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Range: }} & \mathbf{\{9\}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Constant linear function:** When $m = 0$, $g(x) = 9$. It is a flat horizontal line with domain $(-\infty, \infty)$ and range $\{9\}$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 7, both outputs are 9. Slope is 0, so $g(x) = 9$.

[TA Sora] A horizontal line at height 9! Notice that a constant function IS a function, unlike a vertical line!
