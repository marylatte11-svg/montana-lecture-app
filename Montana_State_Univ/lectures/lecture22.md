### [Slide 1] Section 2.3: Point-Slope Form of a Line
*Unit 2 • Lecture 22 • Section 2.3 (Workbook p. 41)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Point-Slope Form (Workbook p. 41)
$$\mathbf{y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)}$$
- $m$ is the slope.
- $(x_1, y_1)$ is **any point** on the line.
- Can be used to find the equation of **any non-vertical line**!
- Once substituted, distribute $m$ and solve for $y$ to get slope-intercept form $y = mx + b$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Identify slope } m \text{ and given point } (x_1, y_1). \\
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Substitute into } y - y_1 = m(x - x_1). \\
\text{Step 3: } & \text{Distribute } m \text{ across the parentheses.} \\
\text{Step 4: } & \text{Add } y_1 \text{ to both sides to isolate } y = mx + b.
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Minus sign in formula:** $y - y_1$ and $x - x_1$ contain subtraction signs. If $x_1$ is negative, $x - (-3)$ becomes $x + 3$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Section 2.3! If you know the slope and just one point on a line, Point-Slope Form builds the equation every single time.

[TA Sora] It is derived directly from the slope formula by multiplying both sides by $(x - x_1)$!

### [Slide 2] Section 2.3 Example 1: Slope -4/3 and Y-Intercept (0, -3)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 22 • Section 2.3 Example 1 (Workbook p. 41)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 1 (Workbook p. 41)
Find the equation of the line with a $y$-intercept of $(0, -3)$ and a slope of $m = -\frac{4}{3}$.
- Write the equation in slope-intercept form.
- Graph the line on the Cartesian coordinate plane.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Given: } & m = -\frac{4}{3}, \quad b = -3 \\[0.5em]
\text{Direct substitution: } & y = mx + b \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Equation: }} & \mathbf{y = -\frac{4}{3}x - 3}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Immediate answer when given y-intercept:** If the point is $(0, b)$, you don't even need point-slope! Just drop $m$ and $b$ directly into $y = mx + b$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 1, we are given the $y$-intercept $(0, -3)$ directly. We can plug $m = -\frac{4}{3}$ and $b = -3$ straight into $y = mx + b$.

[TA Sora] That gives $y = -\frac{4}{3}x - 3$ instantly!

### [Slide 3] Section 2.3 Example 2: Slope -4/3 and X-Intercept (-3, 0)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 22 • Section 2.3 Example 2 (Workbook p. 41)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 2 (Workbook p. 41)
Find the equation of the line with $x$-intercept of $(-3, 0)$ and a slope of $m = -\frac{4}{3}$.
- Can you plug $-3$ in for $b$?
- Use point-slope form $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ to find the correct equation.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Given: } & (x_1, y_1) = (-3, 0), \quad m = -\frac{4}{3} \\[0.5em]
y - y_1 & = m(x - x_1) \\[0.5em]
y - 0 & = -\frac{4}{3}(x - (-3)) \\[0.5em]
y & = -\frac{4}{3}(x + 3) = -\frac{4}{3}x - \left(\frac{4}{3} \cdot 3\right) \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{-\frac{4}{3}x - 4}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**DO NOT confuse x-intercept with y-intercept!** $(-3, 0)$ is an $x$-intercept. The $y$-intercept is $(0, -4)$, NOT $(0, -3)$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Example 2 is a classic exam question. Students see $-3$ and write $y = -\frac{4}{3}x - 3$. Why is that wrong, Sora?

[TA Sora] Because $(-3, 0)$ is on the $x$-axis, not the $y$-axis! Using point-slope reveals that the true $y$-intercept is $-4$, giving $y = -\frac{4}{3}x - 4$!

### [Slide 4] Section 2.3 Example 3 (Step 1): Finding Slope between (2, 3) and (-6, 1)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 22 • Section 2.3 Example 3 (Workbook p. 41)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 3 Step 1 (Workbook p. 41)
Find the equation of the line that contains the points:
$$\mathbf{(2, 3) \quad \text{and} \quad (-6, 1)}$$
First, calculate the slope $m$ using the two points.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Let } & (x_1, y_1) = (2, 3) \quad \text{and} \quad (x_2, y_2) = (-6, 1) \\[0.5em]
m & = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{1 - 3}{-6 - 2} \\[0.5em]
& = \frac{-2}{-8} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{m} & = \mathbf{\frac{1}{4}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Simplifying fractions:** $\frac{-2}{-8}$ simplifies to positive $\frac{1}{4}$. Always reduce slopes before writing equations!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 3, we are given two points but no slope. What is our first mission?

[TA Sora] Calculate slope first: $\frac{1 - 3}{-6 - 2} = \frac{-2}{-8} = \frac{1}{4}$!

### [Slide 5] Section 2.3 Example 3 (Step 2 & Graph): Point-Slope to Slope-Intercept
*Unit 2 • Lecture 22 • Section 2.3 Example 3 Graph (Workbook p. 41)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 3 Step 2 (Workbook p. 41)
Now use $m = \frac{1}{4}$ and point $(2, 3)$ to find the equation in slope-intercept form:
- Apply $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
- Graph the line passing through both $(2, 3)$ and $(-6, 1)$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
y - 3 & = \frac{1}{4}(x - 2) \\[0.5em]
y - 3 & = \frac{1}{4}x - \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{4}x - \frac{1}{2} \\[0.5em]
y & = \frac{1}{4}x - \frac{1}{2} + 3 \quad \left(\text{note: } 3 = \frac{6}{2}\right) \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{\frac{1}{4}x + \frac{5}{2} = \frac{1}{4}x + 2.5}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Either point works:** You could also plug in $(-6, 1)$: $y - 1 = \frac{1}{4}(x + 6) \implies y = \frac{1}{4}x + 2.5$. You get the exact same answer!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Distributing $\frac{1}{4}$ and adding 3 gives $y = \frac{1}{4}x + 2.5$.

[TA Sora] On our coordinate plane, both $(2, 3)$ and $(-6, 1)$ sit squarely on the line, with $y$-intercept at $(0, 2.5)$!

### [Slide 6] Section 2.3 Example 4: Points (1, 7) and (-3, 7)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 22 • Section 2.3 Example 4 (Workbook p. 42)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 4 (Workbook p. 42)
Find the equation of the line that contains the points:
$$\mathbf{(1, 7) \quad \text{and} \quad (-3, 7)}$$
- Notice the coordinates that match!
- Calculate slope $m$ and state the equation directly.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
m & = \frac{7 - 7}{-3 - 1} = \frac{0}{-4} = \mathbf{0} \\[0.5em]
\text{Since } m = 0: & \text{This is a } \mathbf{\text{Horizontal Line (HOY)}}. \\
\mathbf{\text{Equation: }} & \mathbf{y = 7}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Spot identical coordinates:** Whenever both points share the same $y$-value (here $y = 7$), the line is simply $y = 7$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 4, both $y$-coordinates are 7. The slope is 0.

[TA Sora] By HOY, the equation is simply $y = 7$! A flat horizontal line at height 7.

### [Slide 7] Section 2.3 Example 5: Points (2, -8) and (2, 1)
*Unit 2 • Lecture 22 • Section 2.3 Example 5 (Workbook p. 42)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 5 (Workbook p. 42)
Find the equation of the line that contains the points:
$$\mathbf{(2, -8) \quad \text{and} \quad (2, 1)}$$
- Notice the coordinates that match!
- Calculate slope $m$ and state the equation.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
m & = \frac{1 - (-8)}{2 - 2} = \frac{9}{0} = \mathbf{\text{Undefined}} \\[0.5em]
\text{Since slope is undefined: } & \text{This is a } \mathbf{\text{Vertical Line (VUX)}}. \\
\mathbf{\text{Equation: }} & \mathbf{x = 2}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Do not use point-slope for vertical lines!** Because $m$ is undefined, you cannot multiply by $m$. Simply write $x = 2$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 5, both $x$-coordinates are 2. The slope is undefined.

[TA Sora] By VUX, this is a vertical line with equation $x = 2$!
