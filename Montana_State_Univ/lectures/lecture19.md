### [Slide 1] Section 2.1 Example 3B: Intercepts for 4x - 5y = 10
*Unit 2 • Lecture 19 • Section 2.1 Example 3B (Workbook p. 35)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 3B (Workbook p. 35)
Find the $x$- and $y$-intercepts algebraically for:
$$\mathbf{4x - 5y = 10}$$
Then re-write the equation into slope-intercept form and graph the line.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x\text{-intercept: }} & 4x - 5(0) = 10 \implies 4x = 10 \implies \mathbf{x = \frac{10}{4} = \frac{5}{2} = 2.5} \implies \mathbf{\left(\frac{5}{2}, 0\right)} \\[0.6em]
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept: }} & 4(0) - 5y = 10 \implies -5y = 10 \implies \mathbf{y = -2} \implies \mathbf{(0, -2)} \\[0.8em]
\text{Slope-Intercept: } & -5y = -4x + 10 \implies \mathbf{y = \frac{4}{5}x - 2}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Division by negative number:** $-5y = -4x + 10 \implies y = \frac{-4}{-5}x + \frac{10}{-5} = +\frac{4}{5}x - 2$. Watch the signs carefully!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 3B, setting $y=0$ gives $x = \frac{5}{2} = 2.5$, and setting $x=0$ gives $y = -2$.

[TA Sora] Solving for $y$ confirms $y = \frac{4}{5}x - 2$. The slope is positive $\frac{4}{5}$, rising from left to right!

### [Slide 2] Section 2.1 Example 3C: Intercepts for y = -3/2x - 3
*Unit 2 • Lecture 19 • Section 2.1 Example 3C (Workbook p. 36)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 3C (Workbook p. 36)
Find the $x$- and $y$-intercepts algebraically for:
$$\mathbf{y = -\frac{3}{2}x - 3}$$
Then graph the equation on the Cartesian coordinate plane.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept: }} & \text{Directly from equation: } b = -3 \implies \mathbf{(0, -3)} \\[0.6em]
\mathbf{x\text{-intercept: }} & 0 = -\frac{3}{2}x - 3 \implies \frac{3}{2}x = -3 \\
& x = -3 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \mathbf{-2} \implies \mathbf{(-2, 0)} \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m: } & \mathbf{-\frac{3}{2}} \quad (\text{Down 3, Right 2})
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Solving with fractions:** When $\frac{3}{2}x = -3$, multiply both sides by the reciprocal $\frac{2}{3}$: $x = -3 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = -2$.

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 3C, the $y$-intercept is right in front of us: $(0, -3)$. Setting $y=0$ gives $x = -2$.

[TA Sora] We plot $(-2, 0)$ and $(0, -3)$ and connect them with a line with negative slope $-\frac{3}{2}$!

### [Slide 3] Section 2.1 Example 3D: Direct Variation y = 2x
*Unit 2 • Lecture 19 • Section 2.1 Example 3D (Workbook p. 36)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 3D (Workbook p. 36)
Find the $x$- and $y$-intercepts algebraically for:
$$\mathbf{y = 2x}$$
What happens when both intercepts are $(0, 0)$? How do you graph the line?

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept: }} & x = 0 \implies y = 2(0) = 0 \implies \mathbf{(0, 0)} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{x\text{-intercept: }} & y = 0 \implies 2x = 0 \implies x = 0 \implies \mathbf{(0, 0)} \\[0.8em]
\text{Second Point: } & \text{Use slope } m = \frac{2}{1} \text{ from } (0, 0): \\
& \text{Rise } +2, \text{ Run } +1 \implies \mathbf{(1, 2)} \\
& \text{Or choose } x = 2 \implies y = 2(2) = 4 \implies \mathbf{(2, 4)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**When intercepts coincide:** If both intercepts are $(0, 0)$, the intercept method only gives ONE point. You MUST pick another $x$-value (or use slope) to get a second point!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] When $b = 0$, the line passes directly through the origin $(0, 0)$. Both intercepts are the same point!

[TA Sora] Exactly. So we use the slope $m = 2$: start at $(0, 0)$, go UP 2 and RIGHT 1 to find $(1, 2)$!

### [Slide 4] Section 2.1 Example 3E: Horizontal Line y = -2
*Unit 2 • Lecture 19 • Section 2.1 Example 3E (Workbook p. 37)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Horizontal Lines: Example 3E (Workbook p. 37)
Find the intercepts and slope-intercept form for:
$$\mathbf{y = -2}$$
- State the $x$-intercept (if any).
- State the $y$-intercept.
- Identify the slope.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept: }} & x = 0 \implies y = -2 \implies \mathbf{(0, -2)} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{x\text{-intercept: }} & \text{Set } y = 0 \implies 0 = -2 \; (\text{contradiction}) \implies \mathbf{\text{None}} \\[0.5em]
\text{Slope-Intercept: } & y = 0x - 2 \implies \mathbf{m = 0} \\[0.5em]
\text{Orientation: } & \mathbf{\text{Horizontal Line at height } y = -2}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**No x-intercept:** A horizontal line parallel to the $x$-axis never crosses the $x$-axis. It has NO $x$-intercept!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] $y = -2$ has no $x$ variable. That means $y$ is $-2$ for every possible value of $x$.

[TA Sora] The slope is $0$. It never touches the $x$-axis, so there is no $x$-intercept, but the $y$-intercept is $(0, -2)$!

### [Slide 5] Section 2.1 Example 3F: Vertical Line x = 3
*Unit 2 • Lecture 19 • Section 2.1 Example 3F (Workbook p. 37)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Vertical Lines: Example 3F (Workbook p. 37)
Find the intercepts and slope-intercept form for:
$$\mathbf{x = 3}$$
- State the $x$-intercept.
- State the $y$-intercept (if any).
- Can this equation be written in slope-intercept form $y = mx + b$?

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x\text{-intercept: }} & \mathbf{(3, 0)} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept: }} & \text{Set } x = 0 \implies 0 = 3 \; (\text{contradiction}) \implies \mathbf{\text{None}} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope: }} & \mathbf{\text{Undefined (Division by zero in run)}} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Note: }} & \mathbf{\text{Vertical lines CANNOT be written in } y = mx + b!}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Vertical lines are not functions:** Because slope is undefined, you CANNOT write $x = 3$ in $y = mx + b$ format!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 3F, $x = 3$. This is a vertical line. Its slope is undefined because there is zero horizontal run.

[TA Sora] It crosses the $x$-axis at $(3, 0)$ and never crosses the $y$-axis!

### [Slide 6] The Famous 'HOY VUX' Mnemonic for Special Lines
*Unit 2 • Lecture 19 • Section 2.1 (Workbook p. 37)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### The 'HOY VUX' Rule (Workbook p. 37)
How can you permanently remember the equations and slopes of horizontal and vertical lines?
- **H - O - Y:**
  - **H:** Horizontal line
  - **O:** Zero slope ($m = 0$)
  - **Y:** Equation is $\mathbf{y = c}$
- **V - U - X:**
  - **V:** Vertical line
  - **U:** Undefined slope ($m = \text{undefined}$)
  - **X:** Equation is $\mathbf{x = c}$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Mnemonic} & \textbf{Line Type} & \textbf{Slope } (m) & \textbf{Equation Form} & \textbf{Intercepts} \\
\hline
\textbf{HOY} & \text{Horizontal} & 0 & y = c & (0, c), \text{ No } x\text{-int} \\
\hline
\textbf{VUX} & \text{Vertical} & \text{Undefined} & x = c & (c, 0), \text{ No } y\text{-int} \\
\hline
\end{array}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Never say 'no slope':** Say 'Zero slope' for horizontal, and 'Undefined slope' for vertical. 'No slope' is ambiguous and loses exam points!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Sora, share the famous Bozeman student trick for horizontal and vertical lines!

[TA Sora] HOY VUX! HOY: Horizontal, Zero slope, Y equals. VUX: Vertical, Undefined slope, X equals! Memorize HOY VUX and you will never miss these on the exam!

### [Slide 7] Intersecting Special Lines: y = -2 and x = 3
*Unit 2 • Lecture 19 • Section 2.1 Comparison*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Intersection of Horizontal & Vertical Lines
Plot both $y = -2$ (from Example 3E) and $x = 3$ (from Example 3F) on the same coordinate grid:
- State the point where these two perpendicular lines intersect.
- Explain how their coordinates relate to their equations.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Line 1: } & y = -2 \quad (\text{all points have } y = -2) \\
\text{Line 2: } & x = 3 \quad (\text{all points have } x = 3) \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Intersection Point: }} & \mathbf{(3, -2)} \\[0.5em]
\text{Angle of Intersection: } & \mathbf{90^\circ \; (\text{Perpendicular Lines})}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Direct coordinate match:** The intersection of $x = a$ and $y = b$ is ALWAYS the ordered pair $(a, b)$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] When horizontal line $y = -2$ meets vertical line $x = 3$, they cross at exactly $(3, -2)$ at a 90-degree angle.

[TA Sora] Look at the grid: blue line horizontal, pink line vertical, meeting at the gold glowing intersection point $(3, -2)$!
