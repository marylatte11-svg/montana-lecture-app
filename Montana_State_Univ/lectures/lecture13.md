### [Slide 1] Section 1.7: Solving Linear Equations with Fractions — Clearing the LCD
*Unit 1 • Lecture 13 • Section 1.7 (Workbook p. 20)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### The Golden Clearing Fractions Strategy (Workbook p. 20)
$$\text{Reminder: Whatever you do to one side, you MUST do to the other side!}$$
- **Instead of struggling with fraction arithmetic**, multiply **EVERY SINGLE TERM on both sides by the LCD**!
- All denominators will cancel out into whole integers in one single step!
- **Key equivalence:** $\frac{3}{4}x = \frac{3x}{4}$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Find the LCD of all fractions in the entire equation.} \\
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Multiply every term on both sides by this LCD.} \\
\text{Step 3: } & \text{Cancel denominators: all fractions vanish!} \\
\text{Step 4: } & \text{Solve the resulting integer linear equation.}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Warning:** You must multiply EVERY term by the LCD, including standalone integers with no denominator!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Lecture 13! Today we cover Section 1.7 on pages 20 through 22 of the workbook: Solving Linear Equations with Fractions.

[TA Sora] Students often hate fractions, but in equations, fractions are optional! We can make them vanish in one step by multiplying by the LCD! Let's see Example 1 and 2 on Slide 2.

### [Slide 2] Example 1 & 2: Dividing by Coefficient vs. Clearing LCD
*Unit 1 • Lecture 13 • Section 1.7 Example 1 & 2 (Workbook p. 20)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Two Methods for the Same Equation (Workbook p. 20)
$$\frac{3}{4}x = 15$$
- **Example 1:** Solve by dividing both sides by the coefficient (multiplying by reciprocal $\frac{4}{3}$).
- **Example 2:** Solve by the method of clearing fractions (multiplying both sides by LCD $= 4$).

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Method\ 1\ (Reciprocal):}\quad & \frac{3}{4}x = 15 \\
& x = 15 \div \frac{3}{4} = 15 \cdot \frac{4}{3} = \frac{60}{3} = \mathbf{20} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Method\ 2\ (Clear\ LCD = 4):}\quad & 4 \cdot \left(\frac{3}{4}x\right) = 4 \cdot (15) \\
& 3x = 60 \\
& x = \frac{60}{3} = \mathbf{20}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Both yield 20!** Notice how Method 2 eliminates the fraction immediately, converting it into $3x = 60$.

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 1 and 2, we compare dividing by the fraction versus clearing the LCD. In Method 1, multiplying by $\frac{4}{3}$ gives $x = 20$.

[TA Sora] In Method 2, multiplying both sides by 4 cancels the denominator directly: $3x = 60 \implies x = 20$. Method 2 is much easier when there are multiple fractions!

### [Slide 3] Example 3A & 3B: Simple Fraction Equations
*Unit 1 • Lecture 13 • Section 1.7 Example 3A & 3B (Workbook p. 20)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Use the method of clearing fractions to solve (Workbook p. 20)
$$\text{A. } \frac{x}{-5} = 10$$
$$\text{B. } \frac{1}{2}y + \frac{3}{4} = 10$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & \text{Multiply both sides by } -5: \\
& -5 \cdot \left(\frac{x}{-5}\right) = -5 \cdot (10) \\
& \mathbf{x = -50} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & \text{Denominators: } 2, 4 \implies \mathbf{\text{LCD} = 4} \\
& 4 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}y\right) + 4 \cdot \left(\frac{3}{4}\right) = 4 \cdot (10) \\
& 2y + 3 = 40 \\
& 2y = 37 \implies \mathbf{y = \frac{37}{2}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Don't forget the standalone 10!** In 3B, $4 \cdot 10 = 40$. A common mistake is writing $2y + 3 = 10$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 3A, multiplying both sides by $-5$ gives $x = -50$.

[TA Sora] In 3B, the LCD between 2 and 4 is 4. Multiply EVERY term by 4: $4(\frac{1}{2}y) + 4(\frac{3}{4}) = 4(10) \implies 2y + 3 = 40 \implies y = \frac{37}{2}$!

### [Slide 4] Example 3C & 3D: Multi-Denominator Clearing
*Unit 1 • Lecture 13 • Section 1.7 Example 3C & 3D (Workbook p. 21)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Use clearing fractions to solve (Workbook p. 21)
$$\text{C. } \frac{1}{2}x - \frac{2}{7} = \frac{5}{4} - x$$
$$\text{D. } \frac{3}{5} + \frac{x}{5} = 18$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & \text{Denominators: } 2, 7, 4 \implies \mathbf{\text{LCD} = 28} \\
& 28 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}x\right) - 28 \cdot \left(\frac{2}{7}\right) = 28 \cdot \left(\frac{5}{4}\right) - 28 \cdot (x) \\
& 14x - 8 = 35 - 28x \\
& 14x + 28x = 35 + 8 \\
& 42x = 43 \implies \mathbf{x = \frac{43}{42}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & \text{Denominators are both } 5 \implies \mathbf{\text{LCD} = 5} \\
& 5 \cdot \left(\frac{3}{5}\right) + 5 \cdot \left(\frac{x}{5}\right) = 5 \cdot (18) \\
& 3 + x = 90 \implies \mathbf{x = 87}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Multiplying Variables:** In 3C, $-x$ must also be multiplied by 28, becoming $-28x$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 3C, denominators 2, 7, 4 give LCD 28. Multiplying every single term by 28 yields $14x - 8 = 35 - 28x \implies 42x = 43 \implies x = \frac{43}{42}$.

[TA Sora] And in 3D, multiplying by 5 gives $3 + x = 90 \implies x = 87$.

### [Slide 5] Example 3E: Clearing Fractions with Parentheses
*Unit 1 • Lecture 13 • Section 1.7 Example 3E (Workbook p. 21)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Use clearing fractions to solve (Workbook p. 21)
$$\frac{1}{7}(x - 4) + 5 = \frac{1}{2}(3x + 4)$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Denominators: } 7, 2 \implies \mathbf{\text{LCD} = 14} \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Multiply every term by } 14: \\
& 14 \cdot \left[\frac{1}{7}(x - 4)\right] + 14 \cdot (5) = 14 \cdot \left[\frac{1}{2}(3x + 4)\right] \\
& 2(x - 4) + 70 = 7(3x + 4) \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 3: } & \text{Distribute and solve:} \\
& 2x - 8 + 70 = 21x + 28 \\
& 2x + 62 = 21x + 28 \\
& 62 - 28 = 21x - 2x \\
& 34 = 19x \implies \mathbf{x = \frac{34}{19}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Multiplying Terms with Parentheses:** In $14 \cdot \frac{1}{7}(x - 4)$, the 14 only multiplies the fraction $\frac{1}{7}$ to make 2! Do NOT multiply inside the parentheses yet!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 3E, denominators 7 and 2 have LCD 14. Sora, how do we multiply $14 \cdot \frac{1}{7}(x - 4)$?

[TA Sora] Just multiply 14 by $\frac{1}{7}$, which is 2! Leave $(x - 4)$ alone inside for now. And don't forget $14 \cdot 5 = 70$!

[Prof. Park] Distributing gives $2x + 62 = 21x + 28 \implies 19x = 34 \implies x = \frac{34}{19}$.

### [Slide 6] Example 3F & 3G: Fractional Proportions & Compound Terms
*Unit 1 • Lecture 13 • Section 1.7 Example 3F & 3G (Workbook p. 22)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Use clearing fractions to solve (Workbook p. 22)
$$\text{F. } \frac{x - 7}{8} = \frac{5}{6}$$
$$\text{G. } \frac{1}{3}(3m + 1) = \frac{1}{4}(m - 4) + 1$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ F:}\quad & \text{LCD of } 8 \text{ and } 6 \text{ is } 24. \\
& 24 \cdot \left(\frac{x - 7}{8}\right) = 24 \cdot \left(\frac{5}{6}\right) \\
& 3(x - 7) = 4(5) \implies 3x - 21 = 20 \\
& 3x = 41 \implies \mathbf{x = \frac{41}{3}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ G:}\quad & \text{LCD of } 3 \text{ and } 4 \text{ is } 12. \\
& 12 \cdot \left[\frac{1}{3}(3m + 1)\right] = 12 \cdot \left[\frac{1}{4}(m - 4)\right] + 12 \cdot (1) \\
& 4(3m + 1) = 3(m - 4) + 12 \\
& 12m + 4 = 3m - 12 + 12 \implies 12m + 4 = 3m \\
& 9m = -4 \implies \mathbf{m = -\frac{4}{9}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Don't forget the +1 in 3G:** $12 \cdot 1 = 12$. Notice on the right side: $-12 + 12 = 0$, leaving just $3m$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 3F, LCD 24 gives $3(x - 7) = 20 \implies 3x - 21 = 20 \implies x = \frac{41}{3}$.

[TA Sora] And in 3G, LCD 12 gives $4(3m + 1) = 3(m - 4) + 12 \implies 12m + 4 = 3m \implies 9m = -4 \implies m = -\frac{4}{9}$!

[Prof. Park] Excellent! That solves every problem in Section 1.7.
