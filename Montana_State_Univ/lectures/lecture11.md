### [Slide 1] Section 1.6: Solving Linear Equations — Properties of Equality
*Unit 1 • Lecture 11 • Section 1.6 (Workbook p. 18)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### The Golden Rule of Equation Solving
$$\text{Whatever you do to one side of the equation, you MUST do to the other side!}$$
- **Addition/Subtraction Property:** If $a = b$, then $a \pm c = b \pm c$.
- **Multiplication/Division Property:** If $a = b$ and $c \neq 0$, then $ac = bc$ and $\frac{a}{c} = \frac{b}{c}$.
- **Target Goal:** Isolate the variable term on one side and constants on the other.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Simplify each side separately (distribute, combine like terms).} \\
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Use addition/subtraction to move variable terms to one side, numbers to other.} \\
\text{Step 3: } & \text{Multiply or divide by the variable's coefficient to get } x = \text{value}.
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Reminder:** An equation is a balance scale. Never alter one pan without altering the other!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Lecture 11! Today we open to Section 1.6 on page 18 of the workbook: Solving Linear Equations.

[TA Sora] The equal sign is a sacred balance scale. If you subtract 8 from the left side, you must subtract 8 from the right side! Let's solve Example 1 on Slide 2.

### [Slide 2] Example 1A: Two-Step Linear Equation
*Unit 1 • Lecture 11 • Section 1.6 Example 1A (Workbook p. 18)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve the linear equation for the given variable (Workbook p. 18)
$$3x - 8 = -12$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Add } 8 \text{ to both sides to isolate the variable term:} \\
& 3x - 8 + 8 = -12 + 8 \\
& 3x = -4 \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Divide both sides by the coefficient } 3: \\
& \frac{3x}{3} = \frac{-4}{3} \\
& \mathbf{x = -\frac{4}{3}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Fraction Answers are Valid:** Many students panic when they get a fraction like $-\frac{4}{3}$ and think they made a mistake. In college algebra, fractions are authentic real numbers! Do not convert to rounded decimals!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1A: $3x - 8 = -12$. First add 8 to both sides: $-12 + 8 = -4$.

[TA Sora] Then divide by 3: $x = -\frac{4}{3}$. Never fear improper fractions—leave it in simplest fraction form!

### [Slide 3] Example 1B: Negative Coefficient Trap
*Unit 1 • Lecture 11 • Section 1.6 Example 1B (Workbook p. 18)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve the linear equation for the given variable (Workbook p. 18)
$$6 - 4w = 15$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Subtract } 6 \text{ from both sides:} \\
& -4w = 15 - 6 \\
& -4w = 9 \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Divide both sides by } -4 \text{ (including the negative sign!):} \\
& \frac{-4w}{-4} = \frac{9}{-4} \\
& \mathbf{w = -\frac{9}{4}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**The Dropped Negative Sign:** Students often subtract 6 and write $4w = 9$, dropping the minus in front of $4w$. The term is $-4w$, so you MUST divide by $-4$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1B: $6 - 4w = 15$. Sora, what is the biggest mistake students make here?

[TA Sora] Dropping the negative sign! After subtracting 6, they write $4w = 9$ instead of $-4w = 9$!

[Prof. Park] Absolutely. That minus sign belongs to the $4w$. Dividing by $-4$ gives $w = -\frac{9}{4}$.

### [Slide 4] Example 1C & 1D: Combining Like Terms First
*Unit 1 • Lecture 11 • Section 1.6 Example 1C & 1D (Workbook p. 18)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve each equation (Workbook p. 18)
$$\text{C. } y + 4 + 5y - 9 = 87$$
$$\text{D. } 6r - 3r + 94 + 2r = 18$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & \text{Combine like terms on left side first:} \\
& (y + 5y) + (4 - 9) = 87 \implies 6y - 5 = 87 \\
& 6y = 87 + 5 = 92 \\
& y = \frac{92}{6} = \mathbf{\frac{46}{3}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & \text{Combine like terms on left side:} \\
& (6r - 3r + 2r) + 94 = 18 \implies 5r + 94 = 18 \\
& 5r = 18 - 94 = -76 \\
& \mathbf{r = -\frac{76}{5}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Reduce Fractions Fully:** In Part C, $\frac{92}{6}$ can both be divided by 2 to give $\frac{46}{3}$. Always simplify your final fraction!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 1C, combine terms on the left first: $y + 5y = 6y$ and $4 - 9 = -5$. So $6y - 5 = 87 \implies 6y = 92 \implies y = \frac{46}{3}$.

[TA Sora] In 1D, $(6r - 3r + 2r) = 5r$. Then $5r + 94 = 18 \implies 5r = -76 \implies r = -\frac{76}{5}$!

### [Slide 5] Example 2A & 2B: Equations with Distributive Property
*Unit 1 • Lecture 11 • Section 1.6 Example 2A & 2B (Workbook p. 18)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Solve each equation. Write answer in simplest fraction form (Workbook p. 18)
$$\text{A. } 10(a - 5) + 2 = -3$$
$$\text{B. } 6(x - 2) - 8 = 3x - 20$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ A:}\quad & 10a - 50 + 2 = -3 \\
& 10a - 48 = -3 \implies 10a = 45 \\
& a = \frac{45}{10} = \mathbf{\frac{9}{2}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ B:}\quad & 6x - 12 - 8 = 3x - 20 \\
& 6x - 20 = 3x - 20 \\
& 6x - 3x = -20 + 20 \\
& 3x = 0 \implies \mathbf{x = 0}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**The Zero Solution Trap in 2B:** $x = 0$ is a VALID solution! Zero is a number! It does NOT mean 'no solution'!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 2A, distribute 10: $10a - 50 + 2 = -3 \implies 10a - 48 = -3 \implies 10a = 45 \implies a = \frac{9}{2}$.

[TA Sora] And in 2B, $3x = 0$ means $x = 0$. Please note: $x = 0$ is a perfectly valid number! Do not write 'no solution'!
