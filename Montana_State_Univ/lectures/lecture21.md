### [Slide 1] Parallel & Perpendicular Lines: Definitions & Slopes
*Unit 2 • Lecture 21 • Section 2.2 (Workbook p. 40)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Parallel & Perpendicular Lines (Workbook p. 40)
- **Parallel Lines:** Two lines that never intersect in a plane.
  $$\mathbf{m_1 = m_2 \quad \text{and} \quad b_1 \neq b_2}$$
  *(Same slope, different $y$-intercepts)*
- **Perpendicular Lines:** Two lines that intersect at a $90^\circ$ right angle.
  $$\mathbf{m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1 \iff m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}}$$
  *(Slopes are opposite reciprocals)*

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Parallel: } & m_1 = \frac{2}{3} \implies m_2 = \frac{2}{3} \\[0.5em]
\text{Perpendicular: } & m_1 = \frac{2}{3} \implies m_2 = -\frac{3}{2} \quad (\text{flip fraction and negate sign})
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Both conditions for perpendicular:** Opposite reciprocal means TWO changes: change the sign (positive $\leftrightarrow$ negative) AND flip the fraction upside-down!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Lecture 21. How do we test if two lines are parallel or perpendicular without graphing them first?

[TA Sora] By comparing their slopes! Parallel lines have identical slopes. Perpendicular lines have opposite reciprocal slopes whose product is $-1$!

### [Slide 2] Section 2.2 Example 3: Comparing Slopes
*Unit 2 • Lecture 21 • Section 2.2 Example 3 (Workbook p. 40)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 3 (Workbook p. 40)
Determine if the two lines with the given slopes are **parallel, perpendicular, or neither**:
$$\mathbf{m_1 = \frac{2}{5} \quad \text{and} \quad m_2 = -\frac{5}{2}}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Compare slopes: } & m_1 = \frac{2}{5}, \quad m_2 = -\frac{5}{2} \\[0.5em]
\text{Check parallel: } & \frac{2}{5} \neq -\frac{5}{2} \implies \text{Not parallel} \\[0.5em]
\text{Check product: } & m_1 \cdot m_2 = \left(\frac{2}{5}\right) \left(-\frac{5}{2}\right) = -\frac{10}{10} = \mathbf{-1} \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Conclusion: }} & \mathbf{\text{The lines are Perpendicular.}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Neither case:** If $m_1 = \frac{2}{5}$ and $m_2 = \frac{5}{2}$ (same sign), they are NEITHER, because the product is $+1$, not $-1$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 3, $\frac{2}{5}$ and $-\frac{5}{2}$ are opposite reciprocals. When multiplied, they give $-1$.

[TA Sora] Therefore, the two lines meet at a perfect 90-degree right angle and are perpendicular!

### [Slide 3] Section 2.2 Example 4 (Step 1): Solving Line 1
*Unit 2 • Lecture 21 • Section 2.2 Example 4 (Workbook p. 40)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 4 (Workbook p. 40)
Determine if the two lines are parallel, perpendicular, or neither:
$$\text{Line 1: } \mathbf{x + y = 5}$$
$$\text{Line 2: } \mathbf{-2x - 2y = 7}$$
Solve Line 1 for $y$ to determine its slope $m_1$ and $y$-intercept $b_1$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
x + y & = 5 \\[0.5em]
y & = -x + 5 \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m_1: } & \mathbf{-1} \\
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept } b_1: } & \mathbf{(0, 5)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Implicit coefficient of 1:** In $y = -x + 5$, the slope is $m = -1$, NOT $0$ or just the minus sign!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 4, we must convert both standard equations into slope-intercept form.

[TA Sora] Line 1 solves immediately to $y = -x + 5$. The slope is $m_1 = -1$.

### [Slide 4] Section 2.2 Example 4 (Step 2): Solving Line 2
*Unit 2 • Lecture 21 • Section 2.2 Example 4 (Workbook p. 40)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 4 Step 2 (Workbook p. 40)
Now solve Line 2 for $y$:
$$\mathbf{-2x - 2y = 7}$$
- Add $2x$ to both sides.
- Divide each term by $-2$.
- Identify slope $m_2$ and $y$-intercept $b_2$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
-2x - 2y & = 7 \\[0.5em]
-2y & = 2x + 7 \quad (\text{add } 2x) \\[0.5em]
y & = \frac{2x + 7}{-2} = \frac{2}{-2}x + \frac{7}{-2} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{-x - \frac{7}{2} = -x - 3.5} \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m_2: } & \mathbf{-1} \\
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept } b_2: } & \mathbf{\left(0, -\frac{7}{2}\right) = (0, -3.5)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Watch signs during division:** $2x / (-2) = -1x$, and $7 / (-2) = -3.5$.

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] For Line 2, dividing by $-2$ gives $y = -x - 3.5$. Its slope is also $-1$!

[TA Sora] Both lines have slope $-1$, but Line 1 has intercept $(0, 5)$ and Line 2 has intercept $(0, -3.5)$.

### [Slide 5] Section 2.2 Example 4 (Conclusion & Graph): Parallel Lines
*Unit 2 • Lecture 21 • Section 2.2 Example 4 Graph (Workbook p. 40)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 4 Conclusion & Graph
Compare slopes $m_1 = -1$ and $m_2 = -1$ with intercepts $b_1 = 5$ and $b_2 = -3.5$:
- State the final relationship.
- Visualize both lines on the Cartesian plane.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
m_1 = -1 & = m_2 = -1 \quad (\text{slopes are identical}) \\
b_1 = 5 & \neq b_2 = -3.5 \quad (\text{different } y\text{-intercepts}) \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Conclusion: }} & \mathbf{\text{The two lines are strictly PARALLEL.}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**What if both slopes and intercepts were identical?** If $m_1 = m_2$ AND $b_1 = b_2$, the lines would be the SAME line (coincident), not parallel!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Look at the graph. Both lines slant downward at the exact same rate. They will never touch.

[TA Sora] The blue line is $x+y=5$, and the pink line is $-2x-2y=7$. A classic pair of parallel lines!

### [Slide 6] Section 2.2 Example 5 (Step 1): Solving Line 1
*Unit 2 • Lecture 21 • Section 2.2 Example 5 (Workbook p. 40)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 5 (Workbook p. 40)
Determine if the two lines are parallel, perpendicular, or neither:
$$\text{Line 1: } \mathbf{2y = x - 2}$$
$$\text{Line 2: } \mathbf{y = -2x - 4}$$
Solve Line 1 for $y$ and determine its slope $m_1$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
2y & = x - 2 \\[0.5em]
y & = \frac{1}{2}x - \frac{2}{2} \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}x - 1} \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m_1: } & \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}} \\
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept } b_1: } & \mathbf{(0, -1)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Coefficient in front of x:** When dividing $x$ by $2$, the coefficient is $\frac{1}{2}$, NOT $2$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 5, Line 1 is $2y = x - 2$. Dividing by 2 yields $y = \frac{1}{2}x - 1$.

[TA Sora] Slope $m_1 = \frac{1}{2}$. Now let's look at Line 2!

### [Slide 7] Section 2.2 Example 5 (Step 2 & Graph): Perpendicular Lines
*Unit 2 • Lecture 21 • Section 2.2 Example 5 Graph (Workbook p. 40)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 5 Conclusion & Graph (Workbook p. 40)
Compare the slopes:
$$\mathbf{m_1 = \frac{1}{2} \quad \text{and} \quad m_2 = -2}$$
- Calculate the product $m_1 \cdot m_2$.
- Graph both lines on the Cartesian plane to verify the $90^\circ$ intersection.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
m_1 \cdot m_2 & = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) (-2) = -\frac{2}{2} = \mathbf{-1} \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Conclusion: }} & \mathbf{\text{The lines are strictly PERPENDICULAR.}} \\[0.5em]
\text{Intersection Point: } & \frac{1}{2}x - 1 = -2x - 4 \implies 2.5x = -3 \implies \mathbf{(-1.2, -1.6)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Opposite reciprocals product:** Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular if and only if their slopes multiply to $-1$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Look at the Cartesian grid. The blue line has slope $\frac{1}{2}$, and the pink line has slope $-2$.

[TA Sora] They cross at a crisp right angle ($90^\circ$)! That is the visual hallmark of perpendicular lines.

### [Slide 8] Section 2.2 Mastery: Parallel & Perpendicular Tests
*Unit 2 • Lecture 21 • Section 2.2 Wrap-up*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Master Slope Comparison Rules
- **Parallel:** $m_1 = m_2$ (Same slope, different intercepts)
- **Perpendicular:** $m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$ (Opposite reciprocals)
- **Special case:** Any horizontal line ($m=0$) is perpendicular to any vertical line ($m=\text{undefined}$)
- **Neither:** Any lines that fail both parallel and perpendicular criteria.
