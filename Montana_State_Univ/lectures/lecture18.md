### [Slide 1] Section 2.1: Linear Equations in Two Variables
*Unit 2 • Lecture 18 • Section 2.1 (Workbook p. 33)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Section 2.1 Overview (Workbook p. 33)
- **Standard Form:** $Ax + By = C$
- **Slope-Intercept Form:** $y = mx + b$
  - $m$ is the **slope** (rate of change, $\frac{\text{Rise}}{\text{Run}}$).
  - $(0, b)$ is the **$y$-intercept**.
- In this section, we master two core graphing methods:
  1. **Table of Values Method**
  2. **Intercept Method**

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Standard Form: } & Ax + By = C \\
\text{Slope-Intercept: } & y = mx + b \\
\text{Slope } m: & \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{\text{Rise}}{\text{Run}} \\
\text{Y-Intercept: } & (0, b)
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Converting to $y = mx + b$:** When solving for $y$, always divide EVERY term on both sides by the coefficient of $y$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Section 2.1! Today we focus on straight lines: linear equations in two variables.

[TA Sora] We will learn how to graph them using tables of values and the powerful intercept method!

### [Slide 2] Section 2.1 Example 1 (Part 1): Table for 2x + 3y = 6
*Unit 2 • Lecture 18 • Section 2.1 Example 1 (Workbook p. 33)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 1 (Workbook p. 33)
Graph the following linear equation using a table:
$$\mathbf{2x + 3y = 6}$$
Complete the table by finding the corresponding $y$ when $x = 0$, and finding $x$ when $y = 0$, plus a third checkpoint at $x = 6$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{When } x = 0: & 2(0) + 3y = 6 \implies 3y = 6 \implies \mathbf{y = 2} \implies \mathbf{(0, 2)} \\[0.5em]
\text{When } y = 0: & 2x + 3(0) = 6 \implies 2x = 6 \implies \mathbf{x = 3} \implies \mathbf{(3, 0)} \\[0.5em]
\text{When } x = 6: & 2(6) + 3y = 6 \implies 12 + 3y = 6 \implies 3y = -6 \implies \mathbf{y = -2} \implies \mathbf{(6, -2)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Always use 3 points:** 2 points determine a straight line, but the 3rd point serves as an essential error check!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 1, setting $x=0$ gives the $y$-intercept $(0, 2)$, and setting $y=0$ gives the $x$-intercept $(3, 0)$.

[TA Sora] And setting $x=6$ gives $(6, -2)$. All three points line up perfectly on our coordinate grid!

### [Slide 3] Section 2.1 Example 1 (Part 2): Solving for y
*Unit 2 • Lecture 18 • Section 2.1 Example 1 (Workbook p. 33)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 1 Algebraic Follow-up (Workbook p. 33)
Now solve the equation for $y$:
$$\mathbf{2x + 3y = 6}$$
- Isolate the $y$-term by subtracting $2x$ from both sides.
- Divide each term by $3$ to put into slope-intercept form $y = mx + b$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
2x + 3y & = 6 \\[0.5em]
3y & = -2x + 6 \quad (\text{subtract } 2x) \\[0.5em]
y & = \frac{-2x + 6}{3} \quad (\text{divide by } 3) \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y} & = \mathbf{-\frac{2}{3}x + 2} \\[0.8em]
\text{Slope } m: & \mathbf{-\frac{2}{3}} \quad (\text{Down 2, Right 3}) \\
\text{Y-Intercept: } & \mathbf{(0, 2)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Dividing fractions:** Make sure both $-2x$ and $6$ get divided by $3$: $\frac{6}{3} = 2$, not $6$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Converting $2x + 3y = 6$ into slope-intercept form reveals the slope directly: $m = -\frac{2}{3}$ and $b = 2$.

[TA Sora] That means starting from $(0, 2)$, you go DOWN 2 units and RIGHT 3 units to hit $(3, 0)$!

### [Slide 5] Section 2.1 Example 2A: Slope & Y-Intercept for y = 1/4x - 6
*Unit 2 • Lecture 18 • Section 2.1 Example 2A (Workbook p. 33)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 2A (Workbook p. 33)
Identify the slope and $y$-intercept for:
$$\mathbf{y = \frac{1}{4}x - 6}$$
- State the slope $m$ as a fraction.
- State the $y$-intercept as an ordered pair $(0, b)$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Compare with } y = mx + b: \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m: } & \mathbf{\frac{1}{4}} \quad (\text{Up 1, Right 4}) \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept: }} & \mathbf{(0, -6)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sign of intercept:** The minus sign belongs to the intercept! $b = -6$, so the intercept is $(0, -6)$, NOT $(0, 6)$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 2A, the equation is already in slope-intercept form.

[TA Sora] The coefficient of $x$ is $m = \frac{1}{4}$, and the constant is $-6$, so the $y$-intercept is $(0, -6)$!

### [Slide 6] Section 2.1 Example 2B: Slope & Y-Intercept for 2x - y = 7
*Unit 2 • Lecture 18 • Section 2.1 Example 2B (Workbook p. 33)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 2B (Workbook p. 33)
Identify the slope and $y$-intercept for:
$$\mathbf{2x - y = 7}$$
- Solve for $y$ first by isolating the variable.
- Identify $m$ and $(0, b)$.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
2x - y & = 7 \\[0.5em]
-y & = -2x + 7 \quad (\text{subtract } 2x) \\[0.5em]
y & = 2x - 7 \quad (\text{multiply by } -1) \\[0.8em]
\mathbf{\text{Slope } m: } & \mathbf{2} = \frac{2}{1} \quad (\text{Up 2, Right 1}) \\[0.5em]
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept: }} & \mathbf{(0, -7)}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**The negative sign in front of y:** When subtracting $2x$, don't drop the negative sign on $-y$. Dividing by $-1$ flips both $-2x \rightarrow +2x$ and $7 \rightarrow -7$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 2B, we must solve for $y$ first. $-y = -2x + 7$, so dividing by $-1$ gives $y = 2x - 7$.

[TA Sora] The slope is $2$ (or $\frac{2}{1}$) and the $y$-intercept is $(0, -7)$!

### [Slide 7] Definition of Intercepts on the Cartesian Plane
*Unit 2 • Lecture 18 • Section 2.1 (Workbook p. 34)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Definition of Intercepts (Workbook p. 34)
- **$x$-intercept:** Any point on a graph with a $y$-value of $0$.
  $$\mathbf{(x, 0)} \implies \text{Set } y = 0 \text{ and solve for } x$$
- **$y$-intercept:** Any point on a graph with an $x$-value of $0$.
  $$\mathbf{(0, y)} \implies \text{Set } x = 0 \text{ and solve for } y$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{To find } x\text{-intercept: } & \text{Substitute } y = 0 \text{ into the equation, then solve for } x. \\
\text{To find } y\text{-intercept: } & \text{Substitute } x = 0 \text{ into the equation, then solve for } y.
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Which coordinate is zero?** To find the $x$-intercept, $y=0$. To find the $y$-intercept, $x=0$. It's always the OPPOSITE variable that is set to zero!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Remember this golden rule: the $x$-intercept sits on the $x$-axis, so its height is 0 ($y=0$).

[TA Sora] And the $y$-intercept sits on the $y$-axis, so its horizontal shift is 0 ($x=0$)!

### [Slide 8] Section 2.1 Example 3A: Intercepts for 5x + 2y = 6
*Unit 2 • Lecture 18 • Section 2.1 Example 3A (Workbook p. 34)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Example 3A (Workbook p. 34)
Find the $x$- and $y$-intercepts algebraically for:
$$\mathbf{5x + 2y = 6}$$
Then re-write the equation into slope-intercept form.

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x\text{-intercept: }} & 5x + 2(0) = 6 \implies 5x = 6 \implies \mathbf{x = \frac{6}{5} = 1.2} \implies \mathbf{\left(\frac{6}{5}, 0\right)} \\[0.6em]
\mathbf{y\text{-intercept: }} & 5(0) + 2y = 6 \implies 2y = 6 \implies \mathbf{y = 3} \implies \mathbf{(0, 3)} \\[0.8em]
\text{Slope-Intercept: } & 2y = -5x + 6 \implies \mathbf{y = -\frac{5}{2}x + 3}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Fractional intercepts are valid!** $x = \frac{6}{5}$ is $1.2$. Plot it just past $1$ on the $x$-axis. Fractions are common in algebra!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In Example 3A, the $x$-intercept is a fraction: $\frac{6}{5}$, which is $1.2$.

[TA Sora] Setting $x=0$ yields $y=3$. So our intercepts are $(\frac{6}{5}, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$. Look at how neatly they graph!
