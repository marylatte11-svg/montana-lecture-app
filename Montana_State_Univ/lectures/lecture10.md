### [Slide 1] Section 1.5: Advanced Rational Addition & Subtraction (Part 2)
*Unit 1 • Lecture 10 • Section 1.5 (Workbook p. 17)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Advanced Rational Denominators (Workbook p. 17)
When denominators contain higher variable powers or multiple terms:
1. **Take the highest power of each variable factor** (e.g. $x$ and $x^4 \implies \text{LCD has } x^4$).
2. **Binomial Numerator Trap:** When subtracting fractions with binomial numerators:
$$\frac{A}{C} - \frac{B + D}{C} = \frac{A - (B + D)}{C} = \frac{A - B - D}{C}$$
**The negative sign MUST distribute to every term in the numerator!**

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Crucial Example: } & \frac{5}{x} - \frac{x - 3}{x} = \frac{5 - (x - 3)}{x} = \frac{5 - x + 3}{x} = \mathbf{\frac{8 - x}{x}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Fatal Trap:** Writing $\frac{5 - x - 3}{x}$. You must distribute the subtraction to get $+3$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Welcome to Lecture 10! Today we tackle page 17 of your workbook: Example 3. These are the most rigorous rational expression problems in Unit 1.

[TA Sora] Watch out for two things: highest variable powers in LCD, and distributing the subtraction over binomial numerators! Let's go to Slide 2.

### [Slide 2] Example 3A: Quadratic Monomial Denominator
*Unit 1 • Lecture 10 • Section 1.5 Example 3A (Workbook p. 17)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Add or subtract the expression and simplify (Workbook p. 17)
$$\frac{3}{7x^2} + \frac{5}{14x}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Find LCD:} \\
& \text{Coefficients: } \text{LCM}(7, 14) = 14 \\
& \text{Variables: Highest power between } x^2 \text{ and } x \text{ is } x^2 \implies \mathbf{\text{LCD} = 14x^2} \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Build common denominator:} \\
& \frac{3 \cdot 2}{7x^2 \cdot 2} + \frac{5 \cdot x}{14x \cdot x} = \frac{6}{14x^2} + \frac{5x}{14x^2} \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 3: } & \text{Combine numerators:} \\
&= \mathbf{\frac{6 + 5x}{14x^2}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Power Choice:** In the LCD, choose $x^2$, NOT $x^3$ or $x$! The LCD takes the HIGHEST power present: $14x^2$.

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 3A, we have $7x^2$ and $14x$. Sora, what is the LCD?

[TA Sora] For the numbers 7 and 14, it's 14. For $x^2$ and $x$, we take the higher power, which is $x^2$! So LCD is $14x^2$.

[Prof. Park] Multiply the first fraction by $\frac{2}{2}$ and the second by $\frac{x}{x}$, giving $\frac{6 + 5x}{14x^2}$.

### [Slide 3] Example 3B: Three-Term Rational Expression with 4th Power
*Unit 1 • Lecture 10 • Section 1.5 Example 3B (Workbook p. 17)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Add or subtract the expression and simplify (Workbook p. 17)
$$\frac{2}{x} - \frac{9}{10x^4} + \frac{3}{x}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Notice that } \frac{2}{x} \text{ and } \frac{3}{x} \text{ already have the same denominator!} \\
& \left(\frac{2}{x} + \frac{3}{x}\right) - \frac{9}{10x^4} = \frac{5}{x} - \frac{9}{10x^4} \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Find LCD of } x \text{ and } 10x^4: \quad \mathbf{\text{LCD} = 10x^4} \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 3: } & \text{Convert to common denominator:} \\
& \frac{5 \cdot 10x^3}{x \cdot 10x^3} - \frac{9}{10x^4} = \frac{50x^3}{10x^4} - \frac{9}{10x^4} = \mathbf{\frac{50x^3 - 9}{10x^4}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sora's Shortcut:** Always combine like fractions first! Adding $\frac{2}{x} + \frac{3}{x} = \frac{5}{x}$ immediately turns a 3-fraction problem into a simple 2-fraction problem!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Look at 3B: $\frac{2}{x} - \frac{9}{10x^4} + \frac{3}{x}$. Notice anything special, Sora?

[TA Sora] Yes! $\frac{2}{x}$ and $\frac{3}{x}$ have the exact same denominator! Combine them first to get $\frac{5}{x}$.

[Prof. Park] Brilliant! Now we just have $\frac{5}{x} - \frac{9}{10x^4}$. The LCD is $10x^4$. Multiplying by $10x^3$ gives $\frac{50x^3 - 9}{10x^4}$.

### [Slide 4] Example 3C & 3D: Multi-Term Common Denominators
*Unit 1 • Lecture 10 • Section 1.5 Example 3C & 3D (Workbook p. 17)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Add or subtract the expressions (Workbook p. 17)
$$\text{C. } \frac{2}{3b} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{6b}$$
$$\text{D. } \frac{2}{5r} - \frac{3}{4r} - \frac{7}{10}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{Part\ C:}\quad & \text{Combine } b\text{-terms first: } \frac{2}{3b} + \frac{1}{6b} = \frac{4}{6b} + \frac{1}{6b} = \frac{5}{6b} \\
& \text{Now add } \frac{5}{6b} + \frac{1}{4}. \quad \text{LCD of } 6b \text{ and } 4 \text{ is } 12b. \\
&= \frac{5 \cdot 2}{6b \cdot 2} + \frac{1 \cdot 3b}{4 \cdot 3b} = \mathbf{\frac{10 + 3b}{12b}} \\[1em]
\mathbf{Part\ D:}\quad & \text{Combine } r\text{-terms first: } \frac{2}{5r} - \frac{3}{4r} = \frac{8}{20r} - \frac{15}{20r} = \frac{-7}{20r} \\
& \text{Now subtract } \frac{-7}{20r} - \frac{7}{10}. \quad \text{LCD of } 20r \text{ and } 10 \text{ is } 20r. \\
&= \frac{-7}{20r} - \frac{7 \cdot 2r}{10 \cdot 2r} = \mathbf{\frac{-7 - 14r}{20r}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Sign Caution:** In Part D, $8 - 15 = -7$. Keep that negative sign in front of the 7!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 3C, we combine $\frac{2}{3b} + \frac{1}{6b} = \frac{5}{6b}$, then add $\frac{1}{4}$ using LCD $12b$ to get $\frac{10 + 3b}{12b}$.

[TA Sora] And in 3D, combining the $r$-terms gives $\frac{-7}{20r}$, and subtracting $\frac{14r}{20r}$ gives $\frac{-7 - 14r}{20r}$.

### [Slide 5] Example 3E: Subtracting Fractions with Binomial Numerators
*Unit 1 • Lecture 10 • Section 1.5 Example 3E (Workbook p. 17)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Subtract and simplify fully (Workbook p. 17)
$$\frac{2x - 5}{3x} - \frac{5x + 9}{6x}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Find LCD of } 3x \text{ and } 6x: \quad \mathbf{\text{LCD} = 6x} \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Scale the first fraction by } \frac{2}{2}: \\
& \frac{2(2x - 5)}{6x} - \frac{5x + 9}{6x} = \frac{4x - 10}{6x} - \frac{5x + 9}{6x} \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 3: } & \text{Combine with parentheses to distribute the minus sign!} \\
&= \frac{(4x - 10) - (5x + 9)}{6x} \\
&= \frac{4x - 10 - 5x - 9}{6x} = \mathbf{\frac{-x - 19}{6x}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**EXAM DISASTER ALERT:** Writing $4x - 10 - 5x + 9$. The minus MUST hit both $5x$ and $+9$, turning $+9$ into $-9$! So $-10 - 9 = -19$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] Example 3E is one of the most critical exam questions in all of Unit 1. Look closely at that minus sign between the fractions.

[TA Sora] You MUST put parentheses around $(5x + 9)$ when combining! That minus sign distributes: $-(5x + 9) = -5x - 9$.

[Prof. Park] Exactly. Then $4x - 5x = -x$, and $-10 - 9 = -19$, giving $\frac{-x - 19}{6x}$.

### [Slide 6] Example 3F: Second Binomial Subtraction Master Problem
*Unit 1 • Lecture 10 • Section 1.5 Example 3F (Workbook p. 17)*

#### 📖 Official Workbook Problem
### Subtract and simplify fully (Workbook p. 17)
$$\frac{3y + 7}{8y} - \frac{9y - 11}{4y}$$

#### 💡 Complete Step-by-Step Solution
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Step 1: } & \text{Find LCD of } 8y \text{ and } 4y: \quad \mathbf{\text{LCD} = 8y} \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 2: } & \text{Scale the second fraction by } \frac{2}{2}: \\
& \frac{3y + 7}{8y} - \frac{2(9y - 11)}{8y} = \frac{3y + 7}{8y} - \frac{18y - 22}{8y} \\[0.8em]
\text{Step 3: } & \text{Combine and distribute the subtraction:} \\
&= \frac{(3y + 7) - (18y - 22)}{8y} \\
&= \frac{3y + 7 - 18y + 22}{8y} \\
&= \mathbf{\frac{-15y + 29}{8y}}
\end{aligned}$$

#### ⚠️ Pitfall & Strategy
**Double Negative Alert:** $-(-22) = +22$! So $7 + 22 = +29$! Do not write $7 - 22 = -15$!

#### 🎙️ Lecture Dialogue (Prof. Park & TA Sora)
[Prof. Park] In 3F, the second fraction is multiplied by $\frac{2}{2}$, giving $\frac{18y - 22}{8y}$.

[TA Sora] And when we subtract, $-(-22)$ becomes $+22$! So $7 + 22 = 29$. The final simplified fraction is $\frac{-15y + 29}{8y}$.

[Prof. Park] Phenomenal! That completes every single problem from Section 1.5 in your workbook.
