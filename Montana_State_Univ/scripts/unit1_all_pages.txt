Section 1.0: The Language of Algebra and Arithmetic Review 
Key Terms 
Algebra: The branch of mathematics that uses variables (letters) to represent numbers in 
expressions, equations and formulas. We use algebra because it helps us create generalized 
formulas. For example, we know that the formula for distance traveled is to multiply the rate of 
speed (r) by time (t) (ܦ = ݐݎ)  .
Variable: A letter that represents a number whose value may change (vary) 
 
Constant: A number whose value always stays the same (constant) 
 
Expression: A number, a variable, or a combination of those 
 
Term/s: A constant, or the product (multiplication) of a constant and one or more variables  
When you have more than one term, they are separated by either addition or subtraction. 
 
Factors: The multipliers in a term 
 
Coefficient: The numerical quantity multiplied by the variable in an algebraic expression 
 
Equation: Two expressions connected by an equal sign 
 
Inequality: To show one expression is less than or greater than another expression (<, >,൑, ൒)

Section 1.3: Adding and Subtracting Polynomials; Distributive Property 
Key Terms 
A monomial, or two or more monomials combined by addition or subtraction, is a polynomial. 
Some polynomials have special names, based on the number of terms.  
A monomial is a polynomial with exactly one term.  
A binomial has exactly two terms.  
A trinomial has exactly three terms.  
There are no special names for polynomials with more than three terms. 
Like Terms: When two terms are both constants OR have the exact same variable(s) and 
exponent(s), we say they are like terms. Only the like terms can be combined through addition or 
subtraction. To do so, add or subtract only the coefficients. The variables never change through 
addition or subtraction.

Basic Graphing Strategy 
When graphing, 𝑥𝑥-coordinates are the input values and 𝑦𝑦-coordinates are the output values. The 
graph is the set of all such (𝑥𝑥, 𝑦𝑦) points. Hence, when graphing, choose 𝑥𝑥-values and use the 
algebraic expression of the equation to calculate corresponding 𝑦𝑦-values. Plot enough points to be 
able to determine the overall shape of the graph.  
Definition:  Solution of an Equation Involving 𝒙𝒙 and 𝒚𝒚 
 
An ordered pair (𝑥𝑥, 𝑦𝑦) is a solution to an equation involving 𝑥𝑥 and 𝑦𝑦 if the equation 
is a true statement when the 𝑥𝑥- and 𝑦𝑦- values of the ordered pair are substituted 
into the equation.
